## Question
Under the picture changes that LIBERO-Plus applies, does adding C-RADIOv4 (R) to DINOv3 (D) help less, or hurt more, than adding SigLIP 2 (L), SAM 3 (S) or VGGT-Ω (G)? The paper's D+R drop is measured under LIBERO-Plus perturbations (`literature/liu2026what-table10-composition.csv`; `../../README.md`, Key findings), while our frozen-feature tests used clean frames only (verified, `../2026-10-19-feasibility-tally/README.md`). On clean frames R adds the most action information on top of D (gain +0.037 vs +0.014 to +0.033 for the others; verified, tally README). This test asks whether that changes under perturbation.

Why look at the pair and not each encoder alone: C-RADIOv4 alone holds up about as well as DINOv3 in the paper's per-category Table 1 (camera 62.6 vs 64.2, light 97.3 vs 98.5, background 86.2 vs 83.9, noise 86.6 vs 86.0; verified, `literature/liu2026what-table1-single-source.csv`). The paper gives no per-category numbers for compositions (verified, paper note). So the harm, if it is visible at all in frozen features, has to show up in the pair.

### Main measure: the probe
1. Fit as the tally does (`../../src/diagnostics.py`, verified), refit from the cached clean features with the alpha stored in the tally's result JSONs, because no fitted probe was saved: clean features, per-task-file demo-level split, ridge with alpha tuned on the clean validation demos, refit on train+val, R² on the test demos averaged over the 7 action dims, probes for D alone and D+B (concatenated, standardized with clean train statistics).
2. Apply the fitted probes, unchanged, to the test features of each condition. Alpha and standardization are never re-tuned on perturbed data (test data stays untouched in development, `_standards/07`).
3. Per condition c and probe X: R²_c(X). Define drop_c(X) = R²_clean-rerender(X) − R²_c(X).
4. Per-pair quantity: **extra drop** e_c(B) = drop_c(D+B) − drop_c(D). Positive means adding B makes the pair lose more under perturbation than DINOv3 alone. Equivalent forms: it is also minus the change in gain(B|D) from clean to perturbed. Report also gain_c(B|D) = R²_c(D+B) − R²_c(D) itself.
5. Pooled over types: e(B) = the mean of e_c(B) over the four perturbation types (each type weighted equally).
6. CIs: demo-level bootstrap over the 100 test demos, at least 1,000 draws, the same draws for every probe and condition so that differences between pairs are paired (as in the tally; `../../STANDARDS.md` requires demo-level CIs). The perturbation draw is fixed per demo, so its variation is part of the demo-level spread.

### Secondary measure: feature drift
Primary definition (decided 2026-11-07, second batch; see top; alternatives (a) and (b) below are not used): for encoder E, drift_c(E) = mean over test frames of ||f(x') − f(x)||² divided by the mean over test frames of ||f(x) − mean(f)||², using clean-train standardized global features. Reads as "the fraction of how much this encoder's features normally vary between frames that the perturbation moves them". Dividing by the encoder's own spread is what makes it comparable across encoders with different feature sizes and scales. Single-encoder drift only; pair drift is dropped. With per-dimension standardization, the drift of a concatenation D+B comes out roughly as a dimension-weighted average of drift(D) and drift(B) (reasoning, not tested), so it adds nothing beyond the two single-encoder numbers. Same bootstrap over demos.
Alternatives: (a) 1 − cosine between f(x) and f(x'), divided by the median 1 − cosine between two different random frames of the same encoder; (b) linear CKA between the clean and perturbed feature matrices of the same encoder (scale-free, but a single number per encoder, so no demo-level CI without extra work). Drift is a sanity check: a feature can move a lot without moving the action-relevant part of it (speculative), so drift does not decide anything on its own.

### The "stands out" rule (decided by the user, 2026-11-06/07: P decides, B reported alongside, A not used)
**P (decides)**: D+R stands out if (i) e(R) is the largest of the four partners' e(B), and (ii) the lower end of the 95% paired-bootstrap CI of e(R) minus the next-largest e(B) is above 0.
Reported alongside, not deciding:
- **A**: not used (it was a looser per-type version of P; the user dropped it).
- **B (reported only)**: sign-based, on the level of the gain under perturbation. Pooled gain g(B) = the mean over the four types of gain_c(B|D). B fires if g(R) < 0 with the upper end of its 95% paired-bootstrap CI below 0, and g(B) is not below 0 for the other three partners, meaning each other partner's point estimate is at or above 0 (decided 2026-11-07, Q21: both readings are reported, the point-estimate reading as written and the stricter CI-based reading, and both are fixed here before any test-set result is seen. This applies to the clause "the other three partners are not below 0" only; R's own clause is already CI-based). B tests the gain level under perturbation, which is closest to the paper's delta (D+B vs D under perturbation). P and A test the change from clean. They ask different questions, so they can disagree.
Also reported either way: the full ordering of the four pairs by e(B) beside the paper's ordering L (+1.83) > S (+0.49) > G (+0.40) > R (−5.41) (verified, tally README), with no significance claim. With 4 partners, "R is largest" has a 1 in 4 chance by chance (arithmetic). That figure covers only the ranking part: rule P also has its CI condition and rule B is a sign test, so their chance rates are not 1 in 4 and are not computed here. A hit is a hint (`../../PLAN.md` Next 3).
Note a mismatch to keep in mind: the paper's number per pair is closed-loop success under perturbation; ours is frame-level action regression from a linear probe. They differ in kind (`../../README.md`, Key findings).

### Caveat: probe capacity differs between pairs
The concatenated global probe input is 6,656-d for D+R, 6,144-d for D+G and 4,096-d for D+L and D+S (verified, tally README, Config). A larger e(R) could come partly from the bigger input (a higher-capacity linear probe may fall further when features shift), not from R as such. The tally pre-registered no matched-dimension control, and the direction `PLAN.md` Parked list has "Dimension-matched features in the frozen-feature tests". Decided by the user (2026-11-06/07): add a matched-dimension control, reported beside the full-size comparison, which stays primary (rule P is applied there). (Superseded, decided 2026-11-07, second batch; see top: only C-RADIOv4 and VGGT-Ω are reduced, each to 1,024 per camera, and DINOv3, SigLIP 2 and SAM 3 are left unchanged. The original proposal read:) PCA of every encoder's clean features to one common size, fit on clean train frames only and applied unchanged to the other conditions; the common size is not fixed (it must be at most the smallest encoder's dimension, 1,024 per camera for the D, S and L globals per the tally README, Config, and is to be chosen before any result is seen). The same rules (P decides, B reported) are computed on the matched-dimension results and reported as a separate row.

### Predictions (speculative; confirmed as written by the user 2026-11-07, second batch; see top)
- All pairs lose some probe accuracy under perturbation (e_c(B) is defined relative to D alone, so this is about drop_c(D) > 0). Confidence: fairly high.
- If the paper's harm is visible in frozen features at all, e(R) is largest and rule P fires. Confidence: low. The clean tests showed R's gain at the top of the four, and the target-side test did not flag R either, so a null result (R does not stand out) is at least as likely.
- A competing guess: because R alone is as robust as D in the paper (background, noise slightly better), R's e(B) could be the smallest, not the largest.
- Drift: no confident prediction on the encoder ordering. Weak guess: G (a geometry encoder) moves most under camera changes.
