## Question
Under the picture changes that LIBERO-Plus applies, does adding C-RADIOv4 (R) to DINOv3 (D) help less, or hurt more, than adding SigLIP 2 (L), SAM 3 (S) or VGGT-Ω (G)? The paper's D+R drop is measured under LIBERO-Plus perturbations (`literature/liu2026what-table10-composition.csv`; `../../README.md`, Key findings), while our frozen-feature tests used clean frames only (verified, `../2026-10-19-feasibility-tally/README.md`). On clean frames R adds the most action information on top of D (gain +0.037 vs +0.014 to +0.033 for the others; verified, tally README). This test asks whether that changes under perturbation. [Note 2026-11-09: those clean gains are the tally's, measured with stored frames; they become a separate reference and our clean gains are re-measured, Amendment 2026-11-09 sections 2 and 3.]

Why look at the pair and not each encoder alone: C-RADIOv4 alone holds up about as well as DINOv3 in the paper's per-category Table 1 (camera 62.6 vs 64.2, light 97.3 vs 98.5, background 86.2 vs 83.9, noise 86.6 vs 86.0; verified, `literature/liu2026what-table1-single-source.csv`). The paper gives no per-category numbers for compositions (verified, paper note). So the harm, if it is visible at all in frozen features, has to show up in the pair.

### Main measure: the probe
[Superseded 2026-11-09 (refit from re-rendered train+val features, alpha tuned on re-rendered val; Amendment 2026-11-09 sections 1 and 2). Steps 2 to 6 stay.] 1. Fit as the tally does (`../../src/diagnostics.py`, verified), refit from the cached clean features with the alpha stored in the tally's result JSONs, because no fitted probe was saved: clean features, per-task-file demo-level split, ridge with alpha tuned on the clean validation demos, refit on train+val, R² on the test demos averaged over the 7 action dims, probes for D alone and D+B (concatenated, standardized with clean train statistics).

6. ### Secondary measure: feature drift
Primary definition (decided 2026-11-07, second batch; see top; alternatives (a) and (b) below are not used): for encoder E, drift_c(E) = mean over test frames of ||f(x') − f(x)||² divided by the mean over test frames of ||f(x) − mean(f)||², using clean-train standardized global features [2026-11-09: "clean-train" now means clean re-rendered train frames; Amendment 2026-11-09 section 2]. Reads as "the fraction of how much this encoder's features normally vary between frames that the perturbation moves them". Dividing by the encoder's own spread is what makes it comparable across encoders with different feature sizes and scales. Single-encoder drift only; pair drift is dropped. With per-dimension standardization, the drift of a concatenation D+B comes out roughly as a dimension-weighted average of drift(D) and drift(B) (reasoning, not tested), so it adds nothing beyond the two single-encoder numbers. Same bootstrap over demos.
Alternatives: (a) 1 − cosine between f(x) and f(x'), divided by the median 1 − cosine between two different random frames of the same encoder; (b) linear CKA between the clean and perturbed feature matrices of the same encoder (scale-free, but a single number per encoder, so no demo-level CI without extra work). Drift is a sanity check: a feature can move a lot without moving the action-relevant part of it (speculative), so drift does not decide anything on its own.

### The "stands out" rule (decided by the user, 2026-11-06/07: P decides, B reported alongside, A not used)
**P (decides)**: D+R stands out if (i) e(R) is the largest of the four partners' e(B), and (ii) the lower end of the 95% paired-bootstrap CI of e(R) minus the next-largest e(B) is above 0.
Reported alongside, not deciding:
- **A**: not used (it was a looser per-type version of P; the user dropped it).
- **B (reported only)**: sign-based, on the level of the gain under perturbation. Pooled gain g(B) = the mean over the four types of gain_c(B|D). B fires if g(R) < 0 with the upper end of its 95% paired-bootstrap CI below 0, and g(B) is not below 0 for the other three partners, meaning each other partner's point estimate is at or above 0 (decided 2026-11-07, Q21: both readings are reported, the point-estimate reading as written and the stricter CI-based reading, and both are fixed here before any test-set result is seen. This applies to the clause "the other three partners are not below 0" only; R's own clause is already CI-based). B tests the gain level under perturbation, which is closest to the paper's delta (D+B vs D under perturbation). P and A test the change from clean. They ask different questions, so they can disagree.
Also reported either way: the full ordering of the four pairs by e(B) beside the paper's ordering L (+1.83) > S (+0.49) > G (+0.40) > R (−5.41) (verified, tally README), with no significance claim. With 4 partners, "R is largest" has a 1 in 4 chance by chance (arithmetic). That figure covers only the ranking part: rule P also has its CI condition and rule B is a sign test, so their chance rates are not 1 in 4 and are not computed here. A hit is a hint (`../../PLAN.md` Next 3).

#### 4. Replacement sanity check
Purpose: show the re-rendered data are as learnable as the stored data before any test frame is used, so a broken or degraded render is caught. It runs on val only (no test demo is read in the development phase). Reference value: the stored-trained DINOv3 probe, alpha 0.1, stored val frames, the same n-1 frames per demo: val R2 0.6562 (verified, `probe-report.json`, `val_r2.combined.val_r2_stored`; the tally's own val curve value over all val frames is 0.6538, the 0.0024 difference being the dropped last frames, verified, `stored_val_r2_matches_tally_curve`).
- **A. Level check (the user's example).** Train the DINOv3 probe on re-rendered clean train frames, tune alpha on re-rendered val (tally grid), score val R2 on the re-rendered val frames. The scored value is the val R2 of the train-only fit at the best alpha of the grid (the `tune_alpha` curve, `src/pf_analysis.py:241-260`), not the train+val refit; the reference 0.6562 is likewise a train-only fit scored on val. Pass if it is at least 0.6562 minus a margin m. One-sided: higher than the reference passes and is reported.
  - Two noise scales exist and they are different things. (i) Paired, probe held fixed: the demo-level 95% interval of the val gap between the same stored-trained probe on stored vs re-rendered val frames is [-0.0592, -0.0439], half-width about 0.008 (verified, `results/pf-val-diagnosis/README.md` section B; 100 val demos). It says how precisely a score difference on these 100 val demos is measured when the probe does not change. It does not include variation from retraining the probe on different frames, which is unknown (not computed). (ii) Level, test set: the tally's 95% interval on the DINOv3 probe's test R2 is [0.623, 0.671], half-width about 0.024 (verified, tally README, single-probe table); it is a test-set number for one probe and not a val or paired number.
  - Choosing m: 0.01 (the old gate size, set under the smallest tally gain +0.014): only about 1.3 times the paired half-width (i), leaving little room for retraining variation. 0.025: about 3 times (i) and about the half-width (ii); room for retraining variation. 0.05: about the size of the failed gap, probably too loose to catch a degraded render. These are readings of the two scales, not a derivation.
  - Tradeoff: this guards the absolute level, not the gains (gains of +0.014 to +0.037 are smaller than 0.025, so passing does not show the clean gains agree). Both numbers being compared are best-of-grid on the same 100 val demos, so selection optimism is similar in both (speculative). The user picks m.

**The check, as specified.**
1. Mode `check-consistency` of `src/pf_rerender.py`. It re-renders a fixed set: demo_30 of each of the ten tasks, clean, all frames, both cameras, with the same code path as the main render (render at 256, area shrink to 128, the fixture copy, the Q20 and fixture checks). It compares the uint8 128 x 128 output arrays byte for byte with an earlier run's output for the same demos. A difference in any value of any pixel is a failure: non-zero exit (code 5), before any test frame is drawn.
2. Every render report records: the versions of mujoco, robosuite, numpy, Pillow and the other libraries the render or the noise functions use (h5py, scipy, scikit-image, OpenCV, Wand, ImageMagick, PyOpenGL, PyYAML, Python); the LIBERO-Plus commit and a fingerprint of the LIBERO-Plus source files (`libero/libero/envs/**/*.py` and `task_classification.json`); the GL vendor, renderer and version strings (read after the first render, best effort; the reason is recorded if they cannot be read); the GPU name and driver from `nvidia-smi`; the container image digest if the Job sets `IMAGE_DIGEST`; `MUJOCO_GL`; and the environment bundle's identity. `src/env_bundle.sh` exposes no bundle checksum (verified by reading the script): its key is a local shell variable (first 12 hex of the sha256 of `setup_env.sh`, `setup_sim.sh` and `env_bundle.sh` concatenated) and only `build` prints the tar's sha256. So the report recomputes that key from the three script files (`--env-scripts-dir`) and takes the tar's sha256 from `ENV_BUNDLE_SHA256` if the Job sets it; the Job file (a later task) must set it or accept `null`. `LP_COMMIT` is likewise a non-exported variable in `setup_sim.sh` and the LIBERO-Plus tarball has no `.git`; the report reads the value from that file, and the source fingerprint is the check on the files themselves.
3. Comparison with the earlier run's report: any difference in a library version, the LIBERO-Plus commit or source fingerprint, the bundle key or tar checksum, the image digest or `MUJOCO_GL`, or a field the earlier report did not record, stops the run before anything is rendered. In this strict mode a null or missing value on either side for the mujoco or robosuite version, the image digest, the bundle key or the LIBERO-Plus source fingerprint also stops it (two unknowns are not "equal"); the report-only comparison only lists them as unverified. A main-run render (trainval or test, not a pilot) refuses at startup, before rendering anything, if any of these fields is null or missing in its own environment record, so a phase A Job that forgot `IMAGE_DIGEST` fails at once rather than leaving a reference report that phase B cannot accept. A `--pilot` render may go without (the fields are only recorded). A difference in GPU name, driver or GL strings is recorded in `consistency-report.json` and the pixel comparison decides.
4. The same comparison in report-only mode (`pf_rerender.py compare --report-only`: no rendering, never a non-zero exit for a difference) is what phase A uses to compare its new val frames with the pf-val val frames (Amendment 2026-11-09 section 5), with `--demos val` so that only the val demos are compared (the pf-val root holds val demos only; without the filter the default is the union of both files' demos, where a demo on one side only counts as a difference, which `check-consistency` and the strict `compare` keep). 5. A test-split render (`pf_rerender.py render --split test`) requires `--consistency-ref <phase A output root>` and runs the check first, in the same process. Without it the command refuses (exit 2). Any error in the check exits with code 5, not only a pixel or software difference: a Q20 or state-length failure in its re-render, an unreadable or incomplete reference (for example a missing camera key), a missing report. In all these cases no test demo has been opened and no test environment built; the check itself builds the ten val environments (one per task) for its re-render.
