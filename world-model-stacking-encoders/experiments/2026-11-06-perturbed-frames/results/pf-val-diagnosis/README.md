# pf-val diagnosis: why do re-rendered clean frames score worse on the DINOv3 probe than the stored frames?

Written 2026-11-08 by an implementation worker. Labels: **verified** = run in this session (script and output are in this folder), **recalled** = from memory or a file not re-read now, **speculative** = a guess or an interpretation. No fix is chosen or applied here; the options at the end are for the lead and the user.

## In plain words

The re-rendered frames look the same as the stored ones to the eye, and colour, brightness and position match almost exactly. What differs is fine surface texture. The stored frames show a grainy speckle on shiny or patterned things (the metal bowl, the plate's rim, the stove burner, the dark robot mount), and our renderer draws those surfaces smoothly. The DINOv3 probe was trained on the grainy version, so the smooth version looks slightly "off-distribution" to it and it predicts the action a bit worse. The loss is not one object and not one demo: it is spread over many small texture differences and over every demo we could look at. A probe with stronger regularization is less hurt by it.

**Scope limit that matters.** Only one of the ten tasks (`turn_on_the_stove`) could be analysed. The stored frames of the other nine tasks, and the stored (clean) DINOv3 feature files, are not on the Mac and not on any pod I was allowed to read (the stored features are on the CephFS volume `stacking-encoders-features`, which the eval pod does not mount; the stored HDF5s are downloaded into each Job's local disk and discarded). The one stored file on the Mac (`turn_on_the_stove_demo.hdf5`, sha256 `387fc107...aac73d5`) is byte-identical to the Job's copy (the Job's pod log prints the same sha256; verified). So everything below at pixel and probe level is for that one task, 10 val demos, 843 frames per camera. Whether the other nine tasks behave the same is **not tested**. The Job's own all-task numbers are quoted from its report and are not reproduced here.

## 4. Feature level (stove task; `src/pf_val_diag_features.py`, `src/pf_val_diag_probe.py`, `features-stove.json`, `cosine-stove.png`)

Setup: DINOv3 global features of the stored frames (all 40 train and val demos) and of the re-rendered val frames, both computed on the Mac. A ridge probe (both cameras, standardized with train statistics, `diagnostics.ridge_fit`) is fitted on the 30 stove train demos (2,692 frames) and scored on the 10 val demos (843 frames). This is a single-task probe, so its absolute R2 is not comparable with the Job's all-task probe. 95 % intervals are demo-level bootstraps (1,000 draws, paired against stored).

**Per-frame cosine, stored vs re-rendered features (verified).** Raw cosine: agentview 0.966 (min 0.934), wrist 0.945 (min 0.791). After standardizing with the train mean and spread (which is what the probe sees): agentview 0.589, wrist 0.827. For scale, the same standardized cosine between a stored frame and the stored frame 1 step later is 0.835 (agentview) and 0.844 (wrist), 3 steps later 0.717 and 0.709. So in the probe's own view, a re-rendered frame sits as far from its stored twin as the stored frame does from one a few steps later in time (agentview), or about one step away (wrist). Per demo the standardized cosine is stable (all 10 demos in `features-stove.json`, `by_demo_standardized`); across the demo the agentview value is highest at the start (0.73, 0.71) and about 0.50 to 0.62 in the rest.

**Probe, stove only, combined cameras (verified).**

| condition (agentview+wrist unless noted) | val R2, alpha 0.1 | gap vs stored, 95 % CI | val R2, alpha 1 | gap vs stored, 95 % CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stored | 0.579 | 0 | 0.626 | 0 |
| re-rendered (as the Job wrote it) | 0.475 | -0.104 [-0.154, -0.067] | 0.588 | -0.038 [-0.057, -0.018] |

Alpha 0.1 is the tally's alpha for the all-task probe. Alpha 1 is the best of the tally grid on stored val for this stove-only probe (reported as a sensitivity; chosen on stored val only, no re-rendered data used for the choice). Agentview-only probe: stored 0.553 to re-rendered 0.380 (gap -0.173 [-0.220, -0.141]) at alpha 0.1; 0.578 to 0.536 (-0.042) at alpha 1. Wrist-only: 0.613 to 0.548 (-0.065 [-0.092, -0.033]) at alpha 0.1; 0.629 to 0.596 (-0.033) at alpha 1. So the gap reproduces in the single task, both cameras contribute (agentview more), and a stronger penalty cuts it by about two thirds.

**Is the gap concentrated? (verified, combined, alpha 0.1; `gap_location_combined_alpha0.1`).** All 10 val demos are worse on the re-render. Each demo carries between 2.5 % and 18 % of the excess squared error (demo_35 18 %, demo_32 14 %, demo_39 13 %, demo_30 13 %, demo_34 13 %; demo_33 2.5 %). By tenth of the demo, 77 % of the excess falls in tenths 7 to 10, and the 5th tenth is negative (-10 %: the re-render did better there). The 10 % of frames whose prediction moved most hold 23 % of the excess (10 % would be even). So: spread over demos, tilted toward the later half of a demo, only mildly concentrated in particular frames. Per-demo R2 stored vs re-rendered is in the JSON (demo_34 is the weak demo: 0.33 stored, 0.01 re-rendered).

**A consistent offset accounts for most of the loss (verified, oracle diagnostic).** Subtracting the mean feature difference (re-render minus stored, per camera, estimated on the same val frames) from the re-rendered features lifts R2 from 0.475 to 0.547, so the probe gap goes from -0.104 to -0.032: about 70 % of the loss. The mean shift is 53 % of the squared agentview feature shift and 18 % of the wrist one. This is not a fix (the offset is estimated on the evaluation frames); it says the rendering difference acts mostly like a steady push on the features that the probe was not trained for, not like random per-frame noise.

## 5. Cheap tests of specific causes (stove task, no re-rendering; each label is verified, meaning I ran it; all on the 843 val frames except where noted)

Probe used: the combined stove probe at alpha 0.1 (`features-stove.json`, `probes.combined`), gap = R2 minus the stored R2, 95 % CI paired demo bootstrap.

| test | what was changed | result (gap vs stored; unchanged re-render is -0.104) | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global colour match | re-render's per-channel mean and spread set to the stored val frames' (per camera) | -0.096 [-0.145, -0.057] | does not help; the colour offset is already tiny (verified in section 3) |
| Blur the re-render | gaussian sigma 0.7 | -0.641 [-0.875, -0.499] | much worse |
| Sharpen the re-render | unsharp mask sigma 1, amount 1 | -0.412 [-0.598, -0.309] | much worse |
| Paste the stored bowl into the re-render | stored pixels in the bowl's box (agentview only; wrist unchanged) | -0.102 [-0.171, -0.055] | no change: the bowl by itself is not what the probe is reacting to |
| Paste the re-rendered bowl into the stored frame | same box, other direction | +0.008 [0.000, +0.018] | no loss |
| Blur the stored frames to the re-render's fine-detail level | sigma 0.4 for both cameras, the grid value (0.3 to 1.6, step 0.1) closest to the re-render's Laplacian energy (agentview 937 vs 929; wrist 707 vs 796, a slight overshoot; `features-run-info.json`) | -0.008 [-0.021, +0.002] | overall smoothness is not the cause |
| Static mask, stored pixels into the re-render, agentview only | positions with the largest mean difference: top 3 % (7.6 % of pixels), 6 %, 12 %, 25 % | gain +0.076, +0.079, +0.081, +0.079 (so the gap shrinks from -0.104 to about -0.025; the repaired R2 is 0.551 to 0.556 against 0.579 stored) | the whole agentview part of the repair comes from the top 3 % of positions; larger masks add nothing |
| Same mask, split | top-3 % mask inside the bowl box / outside it | repair gain +0.002 inside, +0.080 outside | the repair is the plate rim, burner, robot base, edges, not the bowl |
| Static mask the other way | re-rendered pixels into the stored frame (agentview) | -0.021 (3 %), -0.040 (6 %), -0.089 (12 %), -0.102 (25 %) | damage needs more area than repair (non-additive; speculative why) |
| 4 x 4 cell swap (`regions-stove.json`, `regions-stove.png`; every 3rd val frame, 281 frames; baselines on that subset: stored 0.576, re-render 0.480) | stored cell into re-render, per 32 x 32 cell | largest agentview gains +0.038 (upright y 64-95, x 64-95: the plate and the right part of the bowl), +0.025, +0.021, +0.017; wrist cells at most +0.012 (upper-left), others within +/-0.007; negative cells are within the noise of a 281-frame subset | no single cell dominates; the agentview middle column (bowl, plate, bottle) matters most, the wrist cells add little |

The mask test is the strongest single piece of evidence: it moves the number by about three quarters of the combined gap using 7.6 % of the agentview pixels and nothing from the wrist camera. Blur and sharpen results show the probe is very sensitive to any low-level change, which fits "the probe is brittle off the stored distribution".

Caveats on the tests (verified for the design, speculative for the reading): seams from pasting a region can themselves move features; single-task probe with 2,692 training frames; 10 val demos; the combined all-task probe could react differently; regions are given in raw array coordinates in the JSONs (the arrays are upside-down) and upright in the PNGs.

## 6. Possible causes, best supported first

1. **Fine surface texture (speckle) present in the stored frames and absent in ours, on many objects, not only the bowl.** Evidence: section 3 (difference is high-frequency; top positions are bowl, plate rim, burner, robot base); section 5 (repairing the top 3 % of positions in the agentview recovers about three quarters of the stove gap, the bowl box alone recovers none). Label: the location is verified; "speckle" as the property is the reading of the crops (verified by eye, section 3); why the renderer differs is **speculative**. pf-diag (verified) already ruled out the shrink path and the asset names as the source of the bowl speckle, and showed MuJoCo 3.3.2 and robosuite 1.4.0 in our pod; the version that made the stored HDF5 is not found anywhere in the repo (PLAN, Feasibility 6b: "MuJoCo is not pinned anywhere in the copy" and "The MuJoCo version that made the HDF5 is not found"; verified in that file), so an older MuJoCo or OpenGL path, or different texture files, remain the leading guess.
2. **The probe is brittle to any low-level change, so a small rendering difference costs a lot.** Evidence: blur or sharpen of the re-render costs 0.4 to 0.6 R2 (verified); alpha 1 cuts the stove gap from 0.104 to 0.038 (verified, single-task probe); a constant feature offset explains about 70 % of the loss (verified, oracle). This is how cause 1 turns into a score gap, not an independent cause. Not tested for the all-task probe.
3. **The 256 to 128 area shrink softens edges** (pf-diag: 1.75 vs 1.28 mean difference to stored for shrink vs native 128, verified there). Effect on the probe score untested here (**speculative**); the stove tests suggest softness alone is not what the probe reacts to (stored frames blurred to the same fine-detail level lose 0.008, verified), so a large share of the gap from this cause is unlikely.
4. **Not supported (verified, stove task):** a global colour or brightness difference (section 3, section 5), a position shift of the whole frame (section 3), a frame-timing offset, the bowl alone (section 5), one bad demo (section 4).
5. **Not examined:** whether the other nine tasks show the same gap and the same locations; whether the all-task probe is more or less brittle than the stove-only probe (the Job's gap is 0.051 against 0.104 for the stove-only probe at the same alpha, which is a different probe, so no direct comparison); the wrist finger-tip differences.

## 7. Options (no choice made; cost is rough and for the lead to check)

Each option names what it changes in the experiment (`PLAN.md`: Q2 gate, Q11, Q22 and the "Smoke-test failure" rule send any change back to the user).

1. **Keep the design, change the gate.** Treat the clean re-render as the reference for all perturbation comparisons (they already are, per the 2026-11-08 (b) amendment) and report the stored-vs-re-render gap as a stated limitation instead of a pass or fail. Cost: no compute. Changes: the 0.01 gate (Q2) is dropped or loosened by the user. Risk (speculative): the stored-trained probe is brittle, so perturbation drops may be exaggerated by the same brittleness; this cannot be told from the numbers we have.
2. **Make the probe less brittle.** Use a stronger ridge penalty for the perturbed-frame probes (stove: gap 0.104 to 0.038 at alpha 1, verified, single-task). Cost: a CPU-only probe step on features that already exist (re-rendered features on the PVC and the clean features on CephFS); no render. Changes: probe alphas differ from the tally's, so clean numbers need refitting at the new alpha; the gap would probably still exceed 0.01 (speculative: 0.038 on the stove). If alpha is picked using re-rendered val frames, that choice uses the quantity under test.
3. **Train the probes on re-rendered train frames (PLAN Q11).** Re-render the 30 train demos per task as well (the val render took 255 s of render time in the Job and the pod setup took about 3.5 min per its log; train is three times the demos, so roughly 13 min of render plus setup and the queue; rough), extract all five encoders, refit all probes. The clean control then is the re-render itself and the gate becomes trivial. Cost: one render Job plus five encoder extractions (the tally's extraction of the heavy encoders took hours; recalled, see the tally README). Changes: the clean numbers are no longer the tally's; direct comparison with the tally and with the paper's setting is lost or has to be reported as a second set.
4. **Close the gap at the source: find what makes the stored frames speckled.** Candidates, each testable on a few frames in a small Job: another MuJoCo or robosuite version or an OpenGL backend (EGL vs OSMesa), texture filtering flags, the original LIBERO environment code path, native 128 render at the original settings. pf-diag found no difference in the bowl's asset attributes and the bowl speckle is absent at native 128 in MuJoCo 3.3.2. Cost: a few small GPU or CPU Jobs plus queue; outcome uncertain (the dataset's software versions are not recorded; stored files carry no render metadata I could find). Changes: the render environment, possibly the base image, if one works.
5. **Use probes trained on frames from the same renderer.** The proxy round's Fast-WAM LIBERO frames were regenerated with MuJoCo 3.3.2 (`data/README.md` section 3, read this session); features for DINOv3, C-RADIOv4 and SigLIP 2 are already on the PVC (`/mnt/proxy/feats/*/libero_goal`), SAM 3 and VGGT-Omega would need extraction. It is not known whether its renders are speckle-free (one 256 px sample image of it, `/mnt/proxy/orientation/libero_goal_no_noops_lerobot-image.png`, viewed, shows a mottled bowl; not conclusive at that resolution). Changes: different episodes, labels and filtering ("no_noops") than the tally; comparability with the tally lost.
6. **Feature-space offset correction.** Subtract the stored-minus-re-rendered mean feature offset (measured on paired val frames) from every re-rendered frame before probing: stove oracle 0.104 to 0.032 (verified). Cost: negligible. Changes: an ad hoc calibration applied to perturbed frames whose own offset may differ; not a rendering fix; I would not rely on it without a paired check under perturbation (speculative).
7. **Extend the diagnosis to the other nine tasks before choosing.** Needs the stored frames (about 6 GB from Hugging Face, `yifengzhu-hf/LIBERO-datasets`, one `!` download by the user, because workers cannot fetch outside code) and the stored DINOv3 features (either recompute on the Mac, about 10 minutes per task for DINOv3 on MPS (measured here: 564 s for the stove's 40 train and val demos, both cameras), or a short CPU Job that mounts both volumes). Cost: the download plus about 1.5 hours of Mac compute for the nine tasks. Changes: nothing in the experiment; it tells whether the stove result generalizes and gives the per-task gap the Job report does not have.
