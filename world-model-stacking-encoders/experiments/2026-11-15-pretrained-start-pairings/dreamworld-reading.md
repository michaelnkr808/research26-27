## 2. DreamWorld's method

Setting first. DreamWorld is a text-to-video model, not a robot model. verified: "The proposed framework is established upon the pre-trained Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B [43] architecture. The model is configured to synthesize video sequences comprising 81 frames with a spatial resolution of 480 x 832." (p6, 4.1). It adds three extra feature sources: optical flow, VGGT, and DINOv2 (p3, p4). There is no action expert, no observed-frame conditioning and no robot data.

### 2.1 Encoders, layer and tokens
- Encoders. verified: "DINOv2 [33] extracts robust semantics to define what objects exist ... VGGT [44] models 2D geometric constraints ... Optical Flow [17] encodes dense pixel-level trajectories" (p4, 3.1). Implementation: "the pre-trained RAFT model for optical flow estimation, while semantic and spatial representations are extracted via DINOv2 [33] and VGGT [44]" (p6). Extraction: "we employ visual foundation models to extract offline features" and "we utilize the frozen DINOv2 [33] and VGGT [44] encoders" (p13, Appendix B).
- Layer and tokens: not stated. The paper never says which layer of DINOv2 or VGGT, which DINOv2 size, or whether CLS or register tokens are dropped. verified by absence (searched "layer", "last", "final", "patch", "ViT" and read Section 3.2 and Appendix B). One weak hint: the Extend-VideoREPA motivation experiment uses "three independent projection layers---configured with output dimensions of 768, 2048, and 768---to map the VAE latent into the distinct embedding spaces of DINOv2, VGGT, and VideoMAEv2" (p13, App. A). 768 is the DINOv2 ViT-B width (recalled). This describes the motivation experiment, not the main method's checkpoint, so it is a hint only.
- Optical flow is handled differently from the other two: the flow field is turned into a 3-channel colour picture and passed through the video model's own VAE. verified: "dense displacement fields ... are mapped into a 3-channel space following the VideoJAM protocol [9]. ... The resulting visualizations are subsequently sampled and encoded by a pre-trained 3D causal VAE [43] as videos, yielding the compressed temporal latent z_temporal." (p4, Eq. 4 and text). "This maps the motion dynamics into the native latent space, resulting in a temporal feature dimension of C_temporal = 16." (p13).

### 2.2 Input resolution
- Video resolution only: "480 x 832" (p6), center-cropped and resized (p6, "All video samples are uniformly sampled for 81 frames, and then center-cropped and resized to align with the target resolution of 480 x 832."). Encoder input resolution: not stated.

### 2.3 Bringing features to the VAE grid
- Space and time, in one sentence. verified: "Raw video foundation model (VFM) outputs are spatially interpolated and temporally pooled to align with the latent dimensions." (p4, "Unified Alignment and Integration"). Repeated in p13: "After spatial alignment and temporal pooling".
- Not stated: the kind of interpolation (bilinear or other), whether antialiased, what the pooling operator is (mean or other), which frames go into which latent step, and the encoder's own patch grid and temporal handling. The paper does not say how 81 frames map to the latent steps. (Recalled, not checked: Wan2.1's VAE compresses time by 4 and space by 8, giving 21 steps and a 60 x 104 grid for 81 x 480 x 832; this is my recollection of Wan 2.1, not a statement of this paper.)

### 2.4 Normalization, PCA, scaling
Order of operations as stated in p4: align (interpolate, pool), then one normalization, then PCA, then concatenate.
- Normalization. verified: "we reconcile distributional discrepancies across semantic, temporal, and spatial features via global normalization: F-hat = (F - mu) / (sigma + epsilon) where mu and sigma are the scalar mean and standard deviation across all dimensions, and epsilon = 10^-6." (p4, Eq. 5). So one scalar mean and one scalar standard deviation over all dimensions (channels, time, height, width) of the aligned tensor F in R^(C x T x H x W), not per channel. Literally, F is written with no batch or dataset axis, so the most literal reading is one video's tensor, that is, per-video statistics (speculative reading; the paper does not say so).
- Not stated: over what the scalar statistics are computed (one sample, one video, the whole training set), and whether F is one source's tensor or the joint stack of all sources. The purpose clause "reconcile distributional discrepancies across semantic, temporal, and spatial features" suggests one scalar pair per source (inference from the purpose clause, not stated: a scalar over the joint stack could not reconcile differences between sources). Whether the same step is applied to the flow latent (which already comes from the VAE) is also unclear: the text says it is applied to "semantic, temporal, and spatial features", so by the text the temporal (flow) latent is included (my reading of that clause), but p13 says the flow latent has C_temporal = 16, the native VAE width, so PCA at least is not applied to it there (see the PCA bullet).
- PCA. verified: "we apply PCA [25] to compress F-hat, filtering redundant noise and distilling features into compact latents: z_temporal, z_semantic, and z_spatial." (p4). Dimensions: "we apply PCA to compress the channel dimensions of these features to C_semantic = 8 and C_spatial = 8, respectively." (p13). Choice of 8: "the performance peaks at a dimensionality of 8, achieving a Total Score of 79.48. We observe that lower dimensions (e.g., 2 or 4) result in over-compression ... Conversely, excessively high dimensions retain redundant information, which complicates the joint feature integration within the diffusion backbone" (p9, "Influence of Dimensionality reduction"; the PCA sweep used "a 10k dataset", Figure 5 caption, p9).
- PCA sweep range, read from the Figure 5 bar labels (PDF page 9 viewed as an image): dimensions 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 with Total Scores 79.12, 79.41, 79.48, 79.31, 79.16 (verified from the figure; single runs on a 10k-video subset). The sweep never reaches full encoder width and has no run without PCA, so "excessively high dimensions" rests on 16 and 32 only, with a spread under half a point.
- PCA and the flow latent, a tension between pages: p4 says PCA compresses the normalized F-hat into "z_temporal, z_semantic, and z_spatial" (all three), while p13 gives C_temporal = 16 (the native VAE width) and applies PCA only "to these features to C_semantic = 8 and C_spatial = 8" for DINOv2 and VGGT. The total 16 + 16 + 8 + 8 = 48 (p13) fits p13. So the world channels are 8 each for DINOv2 and VGGT and 16 for the flow latent, not 8 per source. PCA is applied to the normalized F-hat (p4), not to the raw features.
- Not stated: where the PCA is fitted (per video, per batch, over a dataset), whether PCA is whitened, and whether the fitted PCA is saved and reused at inference (at inference the model predicts the PCA coordinates itself, so no encoder is run: see 2.8).
- PCA reference [25] in the paper's reference list is "Boosting generative image modeling via joint image-feature synthesis" (Kouzelis et al., 2026) (p11), which is a paper on joint image and feature generation, not a PCA textbook; this suggests the PCA step is borrowed from that work (speculative, I did not read [25]).
- No other scaling: no per-source learnable scale, no LayerNorm on the world channels, no projector is described (verified by absence).

### 2.5 How features are combined with the VAE latents
- Channel concatenation, VAE first. verified: "we concatenate these priors along the channel dimension to form the consolidated world latent ... Z_world = [z_temporal, z_semantic, z_spatial]" (p4, Eq. 6); then "we concatenate Z_world with the latent" (p4); "the concatenated input z_t = [z_vae, Z_world]_t" (p5). Order: VAE, then flow (16 channels), then DINOv2 (8), then VGGT (8). "VAE first" rests on these equations; the colour legend of the W_in+ column in Figure 2 (p3) was reported by the reviewer to stack DINOv2, VAE, optical flow, VGGT top to bottom, which is a different order (I could not resolve the colours at the resolution I viewed; the schematic is not a spec). Totals: "the noisy video latents C_vae = 16 concatenated with the WorldKnowledge priors, yielding a total input channel dimensionality of C_total = 48." (p13).
- The sequence is not lengthened: world channels share the VAE's token grid, so the number of tokens is unchanged (this follows from channel concatenation; the paper does not say it in these words, labelled as consequence, not quote).

### 2.6 Widening the input and output layers; initialization
- verified: "we expand the linear projection layers to accommodate inputs from both the denoising stream and the world knowledge stream. We extend the pre-trained input projection layer W_in to accommodate the concatenated input z_t = [z_vae, Z_world]_t. The resulting projection layer is defined as: W_in+ = [W_in, 0] in R^((C_vae + C_world) x D). The weights corresponding to Z_world are initialized to zero, ensuring that the initial model behavior exactly matches the original pre-trained Wan2.1. This design stabilizes training by preventing abrupt interference from high-dimensional world features, while allowing their influence to be gradually learned through optimization. Symmetrically, the output projection is expanded to W_out+ in R^(D x (C_vae + C_world)) to predict a joint velocity field" (p5, Eq. 7 and text).
- Appendix: "the newly introduced expanded projection layers are initialized to zero to strictly preserve the initial generative distribution" (p13). The text and Eq. 7 show the original weights kept and only the new slice zero; for the output side the paper says "Symmetrically" and, in the appendix, that the expanded layers are initialized to zero. Whether this means the whole new output block or only the new rows, and whether the output bias is also zero-padded, is not stated (my reading: the pretrained VAE output rows are kept and only the new rows are zero, by analogy with Eq. 7; speculative).
- How the output is read: the joint velocity is split by channel into the four parts (Eq. 8, p5).

### 2.7 Noising and prediction of the features
- verified: Figure 2 caption: "Expert models extract multimodal features, which are noised, concatenated, and fused via a linear layer W_in+. A subsequent layer W_out+ jointly predicts appearance and world knowledge" (p3). Training objective: "The model is optimized using a Flow Matching objective defined over the joint velocity field. The total loss is formulated as a weighted sum of modality-specific flow matching terms" (p5, Eq. 9), "each individual loss term L_k ... quantifies the flow matching error by computing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted velocity component z-hat_k and the target conditional flow u_t(k), which denotes the target velocity field pointing to the ground truth feature z_k." (p5).
- The paper's flow-matching setup is z_t = t z_1 + (1 - t) z_0, target z_1 - z_0 (p4, Eqs. 1 and 2); z_t in Eq. 9 and in the input, with a single t, is the concatenated tensor (p5). So the VAE channels and the world channels appear to share one timestep and be noised as one tensor: this is an inference from Eqs. 1, 8 and the Figure 2 caption, not a sentence in the paper (speculative). The paper does not say whether the noise for world channels is drawn independently from the VAE noise, though a single Gaussian z_1 over the joint latent is stated at inference: "the joint latent [z_vae, Z_world] is initialized from a standard Gaussian distribution" (p5, 3.4).
- No clean/observed split: the model is text-to-video, so no frame or feature is kept clean (verified by absence; the Figure 2 caption says the features "are noised").

### 2.8 Loss on features, weight and schedule
- verified: "L_total = L_vae + lambda_temp(t) L_temporal + lambda_sem(t) L_semantic + lambda_spa(t) L_spatial" (p5, Eq. 9). The weights follow Consistent Constraint Annealing: "CCA gradually relaxes these weights to zero ... lambda(t) = lambda_base . (1/2) [1 + cos(t / T_total . pi)] where lambda_base = 0.2 denotes the initial constraint intensity and T_total is the total training duration." (p5, Eq. 10). So the VAE term has weight 1 and each world term starts at 0.2 and falls to 0 along a cosine over the whole training.
- Ablation of the schedule (Table E.1, p15), numbers as printed: "Fixed World-Loss Weight" 80.76 / 67.85 / 78.19 (Quality / Semantic / Overall); "Increasing Schedule" 81.13 / 68.02 / 78.51; "Decreasing Schedule (Ours)" 83.49 / 70.89 / 80.97; "No World Loss (Baseline)" 81.26 / 68.47 / 78.71. Read plainly: in this paper's setting a fixed feature-loss weight scored below the baseline without any feature loss, and the decaying weight scored above it (single runs on VBench, one stated seed 42, no repeats or intervals). Caveat, a reading of the printed numbers: the "Decreasing Schedule (Ours)" row equals the full multi-source-guided score of 80.97 (Table E.2, p15, below), and the paper does not say whether the "Fixed" and "Increasing" rows used guidance, so the schedule comparison is confounded with inference guidance as printed.
- Lambda sweep: "the performance of both metrics peaks at lambda = 0.2. We observe that lower values fail to effectively enforce the physical and semantic constraints, whereas excessive weights interfere with the diffusion backbone's distribution" (p16, "Influence of lambda"; Figure 7, p15, x axis 0.1 to 0.5). The sweep never reaches a weight of 1.0, the value liu2026what states (p16), so DreamWorld has no direct evidence about a constant weight of 1.0.
- Warmup: "The learning rate is set to 1e - 5 with seed 42, utilizing a linear warmup for the first 400 steps followed by a consistency-constrained annealing strategy." (p13). It is ambiguous whether the 400-step warmup applies to the learning rate or to the world-loss weight; the sentence sits in the learning-rate sentence but the "followed by" clause is about the CCA schedule. Eq. 10 as printed has no warmup term.
- Per-source weights are all the same lambda_base (the paper writes lambda_temp, lambda_sem, lambda_spa as separate symbols but gives one lambda_base = 0.2; whether they differ is not stated, speculative reading).

### 2.9 Inference
- verified: "During inference, the joint latent [z_vae, Z_world] is initialized from a standard Gaussian distribution. While training uses expert-derived targets for Z_world, inference relies on self-predicted Z_world maintained along the denoising trajectory." (p5). So no encoder is run at inference and the predicted feature channels are used (this is the same as what liu2026what states at p15). Extra step: Multi-Source Inner-Guidance (Eq. 11, p5), a classifier-free-guidance extension with masked world subspaces, "wtxt = 5 ... wtemp = wsem = wspa = 1" (p5), which costs extra forward passes: "inference time doubles from 2 to 4 minutes per video" (p13). Sampler: "Flow Matching Euler Discrete Scheduler with 50 denoising steps" (p13).
- DreamWorld's reported gain depends on its inference guidance, as printed (Table E.2, p15, columns Latency / VRAM / Overall; a reading of single-run numbers): "No Guidance" Overall 68.84; "Text Guidance Only" 78.13; "DINOv2" 78.82; "DINOv2 + VGGT" 79.71; "Multi-Source (Ours)" 80.97. The fine-tuned baseline without world loss scores 78.71 (Table 1, p6; also the "No World Loss (Baseline)" row of Table E.1, p15). So the trained DreamWorld with text guidance only (78.13) scores slightly below that baseline, and its advantage appears once the masked-channel world guidance is added. The source ablations (Table 5, p7) rows "VGGT Only" and "VGGT + DINOv2" and the Table E.1 rows "Fixed" and "Increasing" do not say whether guidance was on; the full-model rows equal the full multi-source score of 80.97. liu2026what's composition evaluations use "text guidance scale 1.0" (p22, App. E.3) and our rollouts use no guidance.
- liu2026what's own inference description at p15 does not mention guidance or the masked-channel passes (verified by absence), so that part of DreamWorld is not in what liu2026what says it followed.

### 2.10 Training recipe facts that bear on this
- Base model and what is trained: "We initialize the generative backbone with the pre-trained weights of Wan2.1, while the newly introduced expanded projection layers are initialized to zero" (p13). "The model was efficiently fine-tuned using LoRA on a subset of 32k WISA videos, with a total of 2,000 optimization steps. The experiments are conducted on a cluster of 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs with a total batch size of 16." (p6). Figure 2(a) (p3, viewed as an image) marks the "Feature Merge Layer" (W_in+) and the "Feature Project Layer" (W_out+) with the trainable icon, the diffusion blocks with the frozen icon, and a "LoRA" box with the trainable icon, and the encoders, VAE and T5 as frozen. So the figure shows the widened layers trained directly and the backbone adapted through LoRA. This is figure-level evidence only: the text says just "fine-tuned using LoRA".
- Optimizer: "AdamW ... beta1 = 0.9, beta2 = 0.99, and a weight decay of lambda = 0.2 ... learning rate is set to 1e-5 ... BF16 ... gradient checkpointing" (p13).
- Evaluation: video-quality benchmarks (VBench, VBench 2.0, VideoPhy, WorldScore) only; one stated seed (42, p13), single runs, no repeats or intervals. No robot or action task.
- Limits the authors state: "our approach is currently constrained by the computational resources and the diversity of the training datasets." (p9).

### 2.11 How DreamWorld's base and setting differ from a world action model on Wan 2.2
verified from the pages above, unless marked.
- Base model: Wan2.1 T2V 1.3B (p6) versus our Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B first 8 blocks (PLAN, Question section). Latent channels: 16 (p13, C_vae = 16) versus 48 (PLAN row 9: "the 48 VAE channels"; the PLAN's number, not checked against Wan 2.2 here). So the same layer-widening arithmetic applies, but the world channels are 8 each for DINOv2 and VGGT and 16 for the flow latent here (p13) against 1,024 to 1,152 per source in our code.
- Task: text-to-video generation for visual quality versus action prediction in a robot loop with an action expert; DreamWorld has no observed frame held clean, no action tokens, no future-token conditioning (verified by absence).
- Feature size per source: 8 channels each for DINOv2 and VGGT after PCA, 16 for the flow latent (p13) versus the encoders' full widths in our code (PLAN row 1, row 6). liu2026what states the raw DINOv3 output is 14 x 14 x 1,024 per view (p21) but does not state the width or normalization of what enters the channel-wise model, so no comparison with liu2026what is made here.
- Trained how: LoRA on the backbone and fresh widened layers (p6, p13) versus the stage 1 recipe (full fine-tuning is the PLAN's stage 1 recipe; not checked here).
- Data: web-style video clips from a physics dataset (WISA, p6) versus robot demonstrations.
- Sources: a flow latent from the VAE itself, DINOv2 and VGGT, none of which are the encoders in the stage 2 pairings (DINOv3, SigLIP 2, C-RADIOv4).
- Loss: annealed weights (p5) versus constant weight 1.0 in liu2026what (p16: "Unless otherwise specified, auxiliary feature-prediction losses use a weight of 1.0.").

## 4. Limits

How strong is the link from DreamWorld to liu2026what? From the three quotes in item 1:
- Strong for the layout: both state that features are brought to the VAE grid, concatenated on the channel axis with the input and output projections widened, token layout unchanged, and that the future feature channels are predicted with the VAE channels and started from noise at inference with only VAE latents decoded. DreamWorld states the widened layers (p5), the joint noising and prediction (p3, p5) and the noise start at inference (p5). verified.
- Weak or absent for everything else. liu2026what cites DreamWorld only for the "channel-wise integration design" (p15) and the layout sentence (p5). It does not attribute the normalization, PCA, resize method, time pooling, zero init, loss weight or schedule, or guidance to DreamWorld, and its own text differs on two stated points: its auxiliary loss is constant at weight 1.0 (p16, versus annealed 0.2 here), and it keeps observed features clean (p15, which DreamWorld has no counterpart for). On width and normalization liu2026what is silent, so no difference or agreement can be stated: it says the raw DINOv3 output is 14 x 14 x 1,024 per view (p21), in the same paragraph as "each mechanism's transformations of the raw features", and names "Source-specific processing" (p22) without describing it; p16 says the preprocessing "for each mechanism [is] specified above", yet for channel-wise concatenation only grid alignment is specified. liu2026what does not state whether the concatenated features are full width or reduced, normalized or raw. Weak hints toward no reduction, inference from silence only: token-wise concatenation and the feature expert map from "the source dimension C_f" (p15, p16), and PCA appears only in the visualization (p21). So DreamWorld stating something does not show liu2026what did the same. Anything beyond the layout is speculation as to what liu2026what did.
- DreamWorld is a different task (T2V quality), a different backbone (Wan2.1 1.3B), with one stated seed (42, p13), single runs, no repeats or intervals. Its ablations (PCA width, lambda, schedule) are on VBench, not on a robot benchmark. Its PCA sweep (2 to 32 channels, p9) and loss-weight sweep (0.1 to 0.5, p15) never reach full encoder width or a weight of 1.0, so they do not test our setting. Its reported gain over the baseline is tied to its own inference guidance (2.9), which liu2026what's evaluation (text guidance scale 1.0, p22) and our rollouts do not use; the schedule and source ablations do not say whether guidance was on.
- Code and checkpoint: the abstract ends "Code is available at Github." (p1). The word "Github" carries a link annotation in the PDF, extracted from the PDF's annotations (not visited): `https://github.com/ABU121111/DreamWorld` (as printed in the link; the reviewer extracted the same URL). Appendix F says "we release the model and code under a strict Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)" (p16), so a model release is also claimed. No checkpoint URL or name is printed. Nothing was visited or downloaded. The repository might settle what the paper leaves unstated (scope of the normalization statistics, interpolation mode, pooling operator, where PCA is fitted). Whether to read it is the user's decision, and any download goes through the user.

## 5. Implications for the open choices in the stage 2 PLAN

Listed without deciding. "Following DreamWorld" below means only that; whether to do so is the user's call, and the limits in section 4 apply to each point.

- **Normalization (Open item h, row 6).** DreamWorld says a single global scalar mean and standard deviation across all dimensions (Eq. 5, p4), followed by PCA to 8 channels (p13). Our code z-scores each channel with training-set statistics pooled over frames, positions and cameras (`extract_fastwam.py` 172-181, `proxy_wam.py` 194-207). Eq. 5 normalizes the aligned tensor F (C x T x H x W) by one scalar mean and one scalar standard deviation over all its dimensions. Following DreamWorld would mean: replacing the per-channel standardization with a scalar pair, probably one per source (inference from the purpose clause, not stated), and adding PCA to a small common width. Consequences: a per-source scalar would equalize overall scale between sources while leaving the relative scale of channels within a source untouched, unlike our per-channel z-score; since the scale between DINOv3 and C-RADIOv4 is a stage 3 suspect, this is where the two designs differ most. PCA to 8 channels per encoder would remove the width difference between sources (1,024, 1,152, 1,152 raw) and so change the PLAN's "matched channels" control (both pairings would add the same few channels). DreamWorld does not say whether the scalar statistics are per video or dataset-wide (the most literal reading of Eq. 5 is per video, speculative). The PLAN's recommendation hinges on "unless the DreamWorld reading states what was done": DreamWorld states something, but for a different encoder set, and liu2026what is silent on whether it used any normalization or reduction, so the DreamWorld statement cannot be taken as what liu2026what did. DreamWorld has no run without PCA and none at full width (sweep 2 to 32 channels), so it offers no direct evidence about full-width features.
- **Grid resize (row 3).** DreamWorld says "spatially interpolated" and nothing about the kind (p4). So it neither supports nor contradicts our bilinear without antialias. It does not resolve the PLAN's concern that our docstring cites liu2026what's REPA sentence for this step. Following DreamWorld here would require choosing an interpolation by other means.
- **Time matching (row 5).** DreamWorld says "temporally pooled" (p4) and nothing else. It does not state which frames go to which latent step. Our mean over each group of four future frames and an unpooled step 0 is an unconfirmed reading. DreamWorld's setting has no observed frame, so even a stated pooling would not tell us what to do for step 0.
- **Zero init (row 7).** DreamWorld states zero init explicitly and gives the reason (p5, p13). Our code already zero-inits the new input columns and output rows (`pretrained_sizing.py` 227-235). Following DreamWorld changes nothing. Known difference to be aware of, from the PLAN: with zero init the model initially ignores the features and predicts zero for them (PLAN row 7), and DreamWorld says the same, "preventing abrupt interference" (p5).
- **Loss weighting (row 9, no open item number given in what I read).** DreamWorld uses annealed weights, 0.2 down to 0 over training, and reports that a fixed weight did no better than no feature loss in its setting (p5, Table E.1 p15; confounded with guidance as printed, see 2.8). Its lambda sweep covers 0.1 to 0.5 only, so there is no direct evidence about a constant weight of 1.0. Our code and liu2026what use constant weight 1.0 (`proxy_wam.py` 424-433; liu p16). Following DreamWorld would mean per-source weights that start at 0.2 and decay by a cosine to 0 at the end of training, and the feature channels would then carry no loss in the final phase. This is the largest stated difference between DreamWorld and what the PLAN trains, and it bears on stage 2 because a constant, large weight on extra features is a plausible suspect for pairing harm (speculative; not tested by DreamWorld, which tests no pairing).
- **Encoder variant (Open item f, row 1).** DreamWorld says nothing on the variant, the layer or the token set (p4, p6). It does not help the SigLIP 2 variant decision.
- **Pair harm.** DreamWorld ablates adding sources in a video-quality benchmark (Table 5, p7: "VGGT Only", "VGGT + DINOv2", full); the printed numbers rise as sources are added (79.68, 79.98, 80.97), but the full-model row equals the full multi-source-guided score and the paper does not say whether the smaller rows used guidance, so "adding sources helped" is confounded with guidance as printed. It does not test a pairing of two semantic encoders, and says nothing about a pairing that hurts. So it neither supports nor contradicts the paper-reported harm of the DINOv3 plus C-RADIOv4 pair.
- **Not in the PLAN's open list but visible here.** The Multi-Source Inner-Guidance at inference (p5) is not in liu2026what or our code; the world channels are predicted from noise at inference in all three. Our rollouts follow the same joint denoising as liu2026what's p15 sentence (PLAN row 8) but no guidance. DreamWorld's gain over its baseline appears only once the masked-channel guidance is added (Table E.2, p15: text guidance only 78.13 against a 78.71 baseline), while liu2026what uses text guidance scale 1.0 (p22). So DreamWorld's results are not evidence that feature channels help without such guidance. No part of this section recommends a change.

All design decisions above remain the user's.
