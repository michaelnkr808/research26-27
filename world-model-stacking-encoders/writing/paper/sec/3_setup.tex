\paragraph{The source result.}
Liu~\etal~\cite{liu2026what} add frozen visual encoders to a world action model (WAM) built from a video expert initialized from Wan~2.2~\TODO{cite} and a separate action expert, which together predict future video latents and an action chunk from T5 language features~\TODO{cite}.
Each encoder's final-layer token features are aligned to the VAE latent grid and concatenated with the VAE latents along the channel dimension.
The features enter twice: features of the current frame as clean inputs, and features of future frames as noised targets that the model learns to predict, with one flow-matching loss~\TODO{cite} per encoder, summed with weight 1.0.
The model is trained once on 1,712 LIBERO episodes and scored on the 10,030 episodes of LIBERO-Plus, which change the camera, the robot's initial state, the instruction, the lighting, the background, the sensor noise or the object layout, under three evaluation seeds.
Each configuration is one trained checkpoint; the three seeds vary only the evaluation.
In the paper, DINOv3 alone reaches 84.0\% total success on LIBERO-Plus and C-RADIOv4 alone 83.5\%, but the two stacked together reach 78.6\%: below either one alone, and the only one of the seven DINOv3 pairs that falls below DINOv3 alone.
The paper explains its composition results qualitatively, by overlap in pretraining objectives, and notes that C-RADIOv4 distills DINOv3, SigLIP~2 and SAM~3~\cite{liu2026what,ranzinger2026cradiov4}.
The paper's numbers for the four partners we test are listed with our measurements in Sec.~\ref{sec:clean}.

\paragraph{Encoders.}
We test DINOv3 with four of its seven partners in the paper's composition table: C-RADIOv4~\cite{ranzinger2026cradiov4}, SigLIP~2, SAM~3 and VGGT-Omega~\TODO{cite DINOv3, SigLIP 2, SAM 3, VGGT-Omega}.
The paper says only that it uses ``primarily large (L) variants'' and final-layer token features, so the checkpoints are our choice and may differ from the paper's.
We use DINOv3 ViT-L/16 at 224 pixels, C-RADIOv4-SO400M at 448 pixels (C-RADIOv4 has no L variant), SAM~3's vision backbone before its feature-pyramid neck at 1008 pixels, and VGGT-Omega 1B (final aggregator layer) at 512 pixels.
For SigLIP~2, the frozen-feature tests use the Large/16 model and the proxy uses the So400m/14 model, both at 384 pixels.
All encoders stay frozen. Model revisions and licenses are listed in the supplement.

\paragraph{Data.}
The frozen-feature tests use the LIBERO-Goal demonstrations~\TODO{cite LIBERO}: 10 tasks, 500 demonstrations and 63,728 time steps, each step with an agent-view and a wrist image at 128$\times$128 pixels and the 7-dimensional action the human teleoperator recorded.
We split by demonstration within each task (the last 20\% for testing, the 20\% before that for validation, the rest for training; 100 test demonstrations), so no demonstration is split across sets.
The proxy trains on the same 1,712 episodes from the four LIBERO suites as the paper, from Fast-WAM's regenerated release~\TODO{cite Fast-WAM}, at 224$\times$224 pixels per camera.
It is evaluated on all 10,030 LIBERO-Plus episodes~\TODO{cite LIBERO-Plus} under evaluation seeds 41, 42 and 43, as in the paper, with 40 clean LIBERO episodes (one per task) as context.
The perturbed-frame test renders its own frames from LIBERO-Goal states (Sec.~\ref{sec:perturbed}).

\paragraph{Our proxy WAM.}
No code for the source paper's model has been released, so we build a smaller proxy from the joint model of Fast-WAM~\TODO{cite Fast-WAM}: a video transformer and an action transformer with joint attention, in which action tokens attend to video tokens but not the reverse.
Where the paper is specific, the proxy follows it: Wan~2.2 VAE latents of the two camera views placed side by side (48 channels, a 14$\times$14 grid per view), umT5-XXL instruction embeddings as in Fast-WAM, a robot-state token, and each encoder's features concatenated with the VAE latents along channels at every grid cell, both as clean current inputs and as noised future targets, trained by joint flow matching on actions and future features with each encoder's loss weighted 1.0.
The optimizer and schedule are the paper's: AdamW, learning rate $10^{-4}$ with 5\% warm-up and cosine decay to $10^{-6}$, weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping at 1.0, batch 128, 10 epochs, bfloat16.
Two differences cannot be avoided: the proxy starts from random weights, with no pretrained video prior, and it is far smaller (about 32 million parameters with DINOv3, before the change below).
Where the paper is silent, we chose the following: each encoder resizes the 224-pixel frame to its own input size, and its token grid is then resized bilinearly to 14$\times$14 per camera; each encoder's channels are standardized with training-set statistics; future features are averaged over the four frames of each VAE latent step (the paper states this averaging only for a different mechanism, REPA); and batches come from 16-episode buffers instead of a full shuffle.
Any of these choices could change whether stacking hurts.
We train five configurations (no encoder, DINOv3, C-RADIOv4, DINOv3 + C-RADIOv4, and DINOv3 + SigLIP~2), each with training seeds 0, 1 and 2.
Runs with the same seed see the same data order; we do not assume they share initial weights.
Rollouts follow Fast-WAM's LIBERO evaluation and the paper's composition settings: 30 initial waiting steps, replanning every 10 steps from a 32-step action chunk, no action ensembling, 10 denoising steps and a binarized gripper.

\paragraph{Rules fixed in advance.}
For each test with a decision rule (the target-side test, the perturbed-frame test, the control partners and the proxy replication), the rule was committed to our repository before the data it decides were read.
The input-side test fixed its design in advance but had no decision rule.
The exceptions are these, and the supplement gives the dated record of every rule and amendment.
The input-side test ran DINOv3 in 32-bit and its partners in 16-bit floating point, because DINOv3 overflowed in 16-bit.
The baselines next to it in Sec.~\ref{sec:clean} were computed after it ran, and the one that codes the paper's overlap reasoning as a label is not blind to the paper's numbers.
The proxy was made deeper after the first two full-length DINOv3 runs were scored, before any full-length DINOv3 + C-RADIOv4 run.
In the perturbed-frame test, the first validation check failed, so the probes were retrained on our own renders and the gate was set at 0.025 instead of 0.01, and the render tolerance was set after a validation comparison; both happened before any test-set result.
On the target side, the control-partner thresholds are fractions of C-RADIOv4's value, which was already known.
