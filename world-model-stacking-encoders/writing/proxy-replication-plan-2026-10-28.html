Stacking encoders: proxy replication plan

Status as of 2026-10-28, before any training run. Source of truth: experiments/2026-10-27-proxy-calibration/PLAN.md and decision 003 in the project repo. This document gathers them for review.

Summary

We will train a Fast-WAM-based proxy of the paper's world action model (WAM), using the paper's recipe where it is known, from scratch, 3 training seeds for each of 5 encoder configurations, and test every checkpoint on the same fixed set of LIBERO-Plus episodes. The primary question is whether DINOv3 + C-RADIOv4 does worse than DINOv3 alone in this proxy, seed by seed. This is a proxy replication: the proxy has no pretrained Wan 2.2 video prior, is far smaller than the paper's model, and makes its own choices where the paper is silent (feature scaling, temporal averaging of features) that could affect whether stacking hurts, so its result is about the proxy. Its job is to decide whether a higher-fidelity, multi-seed WAM replication is worth running. If the proxy does not reproduce the drop, that does not show the paper's result was training noise.

The code is written and tested locally. Nothing has run on the cluster yet. The expected wall time is about four days if things go smoothly and closer to six if not.

Background and question

Liu et al. 2026 ("What Visual Representations Do World Action Models Need?", arXiv 2610.13024, preprint) add frozen encoder features to a Wan 2.2-based WAM and evaluate on LIBERO-Plus. DINOv3 alone reaches 84.0% on LIBERO-Plus, and C-RADIOv4 alone 83.5%, but DINOv3 + C-RADIOv4 reaches 78.6%, below both. Each configuration in the paper is one trained checkpoint; the three seeds reported are evaluation seeds, not training seeds.

Our earlier cheap diagnostics on frozen features (CKA similarity, conditional probe gain, and a target-side interference test, all on clean libero_goal frames) did not flag D+R as harmful. That leaves two open possibilities we can't tell apart from the paper alone: the drop is a real effect our diagnostics miss, or it depends on things a single checkpoint can't separate from training variability.

Question for this round: does a proxy WAM, trained with 3 seeds per configuration, reproduce the paper's ordering, and is D+R worse than D in the proxy, seed by seed?

The proxy model

The guiding rule, set by the project lead, is to match the paper wherever its setup is known and to flag every place where we had to choose. The proxy is built directly from Fast-WAM's own code (github.com/yuantianyuan01/FastWAM, commit 7faa711, MIT license), whose processed LIBERO data the paper trains on, instantiated at small size with random weights.

Matches the paper or Fast-WAM

  • Formulation: joint video-action denoising, the paper's default (App. B.1). A video expert and an action expert share joint attention; video tokens attend only to video, the first latent frame only to itself, and action tokens attend to all video and action tokens.
  • Base visual stream: frozen Wan 2.2 VAE latents (48 channels, 16x spatial and 4x temporal compression) of the two camera views placed side by side at 224 x 448.
  • Feature integration: channel-wise concatenation, the mechanism used for the paper's composition study (App. B.2, E.1). At each cell of the 14 x 28 latent grid, the input is the VAE latent followed by each encoder's features; one expanded patch embedding and output head cover all channels. Current features stay clean; future features are noised and denoised together with the future VAE latents.
  • Loss: flow matching for video and actions with Fast-WAM's schedulers and timestep weights, plus one future-feature loss per encoder at weight 1.0 (Eq. 9). Padded steps past an episode's end are excluded from the loss.
  • Language: the umT5-XXL encoder used by Wan 2.2, 128 tokens, Fast-WAM's prompt template, precomputed once per instruction; proprioception enters as one extra context token.
  • Training: AdamW, learning rate 1e-4 decaying to 1e-6 (cosine, 5% warmup), weight decay 0.01, betas (0.9, 0.95), gradient clipping 1.0, bf16, batch 128, 10 epochs, action horizon 32, 9 video frames at offsets 0, 4, ..., 32 (Table 6).
  • Data: Fast-WAM's processed LIBERO (Hugging Face yuanty/LIBERO-fastwam, 1,712 episodes over the four suites, the paper's training set), with Fast-WAM's resizing, camera layout, action and proprio min/max normalization, and padding rules.
  • Inference: 10 denoising steps, replanning every 10 steps, 30 initial waiting steps, no action ensembling, text guidance 1.0, binarized gripper (App. E.3); both cameras rotated 180 degrees and the gripper sign inverted, as in Fast-WAM's LIBERO evaluation code.

Differs from the paper (unavoidable)

  • No pretrained video prior. The paper's video expert is initialized from Wan 2.2 (Sec. 3.1); the paper does not say how its separate action expert is initialized. The proxy starts both from random weights.
  • Size. The proxy has roughly 28 to 35 million parameters depending on configuration (video width 384, action width 128, 8 layers, 3 heads of size 128), against billions in the paper. Final size is set after the pilot run.

Our choices where the paper is silent (flagged in any write-up)

  • Encoder input: every encoder sees the same 224 x 224 frame the VAE sees and applies its own resizing; its patch grid is then resized bilinearly to the VAE's 14 x 14 per camera.
  • Temporal alignment of features: each future latent step uses the mean of its four frames' features. The paper states this for its REPA variant; applying it to channel-wise concatenation is our reading. If the paper keeps per-frame features instead, averaging could smooth over a conflict between sources and change whether stacking hurts.
  • Feature scaling: each encoder's channels are z-scored with training-set statistics. The paper does not say how it scales sources before concatenation. If it fed raw features, a source with large values could dominate; our scaling would hide that effect.
  • Batching: batches are drawn from a shuffled buffer of 16 episodes at a time, so each episode is read from disk once per epoch. This is close to, but not the same as, a full shuffle.

Evaluation

  • LIBERO-Plus: one fixed, seeded subset of the 10,030 episodes, stratified by suite and perturbation category (28 cells) and sampled in proportion to each cell's size in the full benchmark, so a plain average over the subset estimates the benchmark's overall score. It is saved to a file with a content hash and reused by every checkpoint. Every selected episode is run under the paper's three evaluation seeds (41, 42 and 43) for every checkpoint, with the same seed IDs across checkpoints, and each episode's score is its success averaged over the three. The subset size follows the sample-size rule in the execution plan, and the subset is drawn after sizing, independently of and excluding the pilot's episodes.
  • Every rollout is saved as its own record: episode id, suite, perturbation category, configuration, training seed, evaluation seed, checkpoint, subset id and success. Aggregates are never the only output, so the paired analysis is always possible.
  • Clean LIBERO is reported as context only; the paper reports it near saturation.

Pre-registered analysis and outcome rule

Amended on 2026-10-28 after outside review, before any training run; the original text is kept in the repo history. Amended again the same day after a second review: a fixed threshold replaces "beyond the seed spread", the storage volume was resized, an image-orientation check was added, and the proxy is described more precisely. All thresholds below, the evaluation seeds, the subset weighting and the sample-size rule were then locked on 2026-10-28, before the pilot, and do not change after it. The pilot is for sizing only: its success rates do not count toward any result.

Primary analysis: for each training seed s, the per-episode paired difference between D+R seed s and D seed s on the same LIBERO-Plus episodes (each episode's success first averaged over evaluation seeds 41, 42 and 43), and its mean. The three seed deltas are reported individually, each with the counts of episodes where D+R scored lower than D, and the reverse. Every comparison in this section uses the same paired per-seed differences, in percentage points, and every report shows each seed's result and the average across seeds. Confidence intervals over episodes describe episode sampling only and are not treated as evidence about training-seed variability. Any such interval resamples whole episodes, each with its three evaluation-seed runs together, never individual runs. Three seeds can show whether a drop is large and consistent in sign; they are not enough for strong claims about training-seed variance, so this round is reported as a small screening result.

Secondary: each configuration's LIBERO-Plus success per seed and averaged over seeds, broken down by perturbation category, and clean LIBERO.

  1. Calibration is a minimum check that the proxy responds to the controls, not proof that it is a good enough copy of the paper's model; the missing Wan 2.2 prior still limits what it can show. It passes only if (a) D minus none is positive in all three seeds and at least +2.7 points on average, and (b) D+L minus D is no lower than -1.35 points on average. The margin in (b) is half the harm threshold, so a loss that would count as harm for D+R can never count as "doesn't hurt" for D+L; the paper's +1.8 for D+L does not set it. If either check fails, the conclusion is "don't interpret the D+R comparison from this proxy", not "D+R is fine" or "the proxy is useless". The margin in (b) is tight with three seeds, so noise alone can fail it; that failure is in the safe direction.
  2. If calibration passes and all three D+R minus D deltas are negative and their average is -2.7 points or lower (half the paper's D+R minus D drop of 5.4 points), the proxy shows a substantial effect. This is a trigger for the higher-fidelity run, not proof that the effect is statistically established. We then compare clean LIBERO with LIBERO-Plus and look by category to separate a robustness failure from a training failure.
  3. Otherwise (any seed's difference at or above zero, or an average above -2.7 points), the result is "the proxy does not show a substantial effect". This does not establish that the paper's result was training noise: the effect may need the pretrained video prior or the full-size model.
  4. Overlap between encoders (C-RADIOv4 is distilled partly from DINOv3) stays a hypothesis in any write-up, not the explanation.

The analysis script refuses to run if two checkpoints were not evaluated on exactly the same episodes. It is tested on synthetic data with known answers: planted per-seed drops are recovered exactly, and identical configurations give exactly zero.

Execution plan

  1. Storage and data: a CephFS volume of about 1 TB (the caches alone total about 756 GB, so a 760 GB volume would leave no room for the dataset, intermediates, logs or checkpoints) on the NRP Nautilus cluster, and the LIBERO dataset downloaded onto it. Headroom checked: the dataset is under 10 GB, and extraction writes one episode at a time with no merge step, so temporary files stay small; about 240 GB stays free for the dataset, T5 cache, checkpoints and logs.
  2. Orientation check: before full extraction, view frames from both cameras in all four suites of the training data to confirm they are upright and need no rotation. Before any evaluation, pass one simulator frame through our rollout preprocessing (the 180-degree rotation) and compare it side by side with a training frame from the same task. A train/eval orientation mismatch would swamp the comparison.
  3. Feature extraction: one small timed shard first, then VAE latents, DINOv3, C-RADIOv4 and SigLIP 2 features for all 1,712 episodes, and the T5 instruction cache. Cached sizes are about 31 GB for the VAE latents and 223 to 251 GB per encoder.
  4. Pilot (sizing only): two short training runs with the same training seed, one DINOv3 and one DINOv3 + C-RADIOv4, with timed rollouts on clean LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus. Both pilot models are evaluated on the same pilot episodes (their own proportional sample) under evaluation seeds 41, 42 and 43. The pilot fixes the model size and the number of GPUs, and sets the subset size by a rule locked in advance: estimate the standard deviation of the paired per-episode difference (DINOv3 + C-RADIOv4 minus DINOv3, each episode averaged over its three evaluation seeds), multiply it by 1.5 because full runs may disagree more than one short run does (equivalently, multiply the variance by 2.25), and choose enough episodes that the standard error of the per-seed paired mean is at most 1.35 percentage points. If that would push evaluation of all 15 checkpoints past roughly 48 hours on at most 8 GPUs, use the largest affordable size and report the precision it actually gives. The final subset is then drawn independently, excluding the pilot episodes, and pilot results never enter the effect analysis. The rollout code is written while extraction runs.
  5. Full round: 15 training runs in two waves of at most 8 GPUs, then evaluation of every checkpoint on the fixed subset, then the analysis above.

All five steps are pre-approved by the project lead and chained automatically: each step starts when the previous one finishes and passes its checks. A failure gets one fix and retry, and a second failure stops the chain. The chain stops and reports to the project lead on any major problem: repeated failures, a cluster-policy problem, an orientation mismatch, or costs far beyond the plan.

Time estimate: extraction takes a few hours, the pilot several hours, training about a day if enough GPUs are free, and rollouts up to about two days (the cap). That gives about four days end to end in the best case and about six with cluster contention or first-run problems.

Checks done so far

  • The proxy's training loss is identical to Fast-WAM's own training loss on the same inputs and random draws.
  • Padding masks, normalization and the attention mask match Fast-WAM's code. Checkpoint resume reproduces an uninterrupted run exactly. The model overfits a single batch (the action loss goes to near zero).
  • Real-data smoke test on one LIBERO episode: VAE latents and all three encoders extract correctly, and all configurations train end to end. These losses are not results.
  • Not yet tested: the T5 encoder with its real weights (11 GB, to run on the cluster), the rollout code, which isn't written yet, and the analysis code's update to the locked protocol (three evaluation seeds per episode, proportional sampling, the calibration verdict and the sizing step), which must pass its tests before the pilot is sized.

Risks and open items

  • The proxy may be too weak to pass calibration without the pretrained video prior. In that case the D+R comparison is not interpreted.
  • The paper does not name its exact Wan 2.2 variant. We follow Fast-WAM's default (TI2V-5B and its VAE).
  • VAE extraction and rollout speed are not yet measured on GPUs; the pilot measures both before the full round.
  • Out of scope for this round: a retrospective, exploratory check of our cheap diagnostics against the paper's 16 composition results. It is a candidate next analysis, not started, and would be suggestive only because the configurations share encoders and each has one checkpoint.
