# Reading list: fusion (combining several vision encoders, and why adding an input can hurt)

This theme covers two things. First, papers that put several vision encoders into one model (multimodal language models, robot policies, and "multi-teacher" distillation that squeezes several encoders into one). Second, papers about why giving a jointly trained model an extra input or an extra loss term can make it worse, which is the same shape of puzzle as DINOv3 + C-RADIOv4 doing worse than either alone.

Date: 2026-11-07. Leads only: nothing here is read or cited yet (_standards/02). Every entry below was found by a search this session, and the arXiv page (or arXiv API record) was opened to check the title. Relevance notes come from the abstract only.

Start here: (1) Encoder Winners Do Not Reliably Transfer Across VLA Backbone Scale, (2) What Makes Training Multi-Modal Classification Networks Hard?, (3) PHI-S: Distribution Balancing for Label-Free Multi-Teacher Distillation.

### 1. Encoder Winners Do Not Reliably Transfer Across VLA Backbone Scale: A Frozen-Backbone Grafting Diagnostic
- Authors: Zeng et al. (2026)
- Venue: arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed (venue not checked)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14153
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q2 measuring what B adds to A | Q3 novelty vs prior selection work | Q5 perturbed scenes
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: It swaps frozen encoders into robot policies on LIBERO and finds the "best encoder" changes with the backbone, so it is a close neighbor of our encoder-selection question and a warning about how much a cheap test can be trusted. Read it to place our work against it (Q3).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 2. What Makes Training Multi-Modal Classification Networks Hard?
- Authors: Wang et al. (2019)
- Venue: CVPR 2020 (as stated in the arXiv page comments)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12681
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q4 fusion-step suspects
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: The abstract says a model given several inputs often does worse than the best single-input model, and blames overfitting at different speeds per input. That is a concrete candidate reason a second encoder can hurt (Q1).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 3. Modality Competition: What Makes Joint Training of Multi-modal Network Fail in Deep Learning? (Provably)
- Authors: Huang et al. (2022)
- Venue: arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed (venue not checked)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12221
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: The abstract proves, in a simplified setting, that inputs compete and the network learns only some of them, which would explain a combined model losing to the best single one. The setting is late fusion and simplified, so check how far it carries to our concatenation (Q1).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 4. PHI-S: Distribution Balancing for Label-Free Multi-Teacher Distillation
- Authors: Ranzinger et al. (2024)
- Venue: arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed (venue not checked)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01680
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q1 why a pair hurts
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: It studies how differences in the scale and spread of each teacher's features, and the loss used, change the result when several encoders are matched at once. That bears directly on our suspects of feature scale and equal loss weights (Q4).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 5. Eagle: Exploring The Design Space for Multimodal LLMs with Mixture of Encoders
- Authors: Shi et al. (2024)
- Venue: arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed (venue not checked)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15998
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q3 novelty vs prior selection work
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: The abstract reports that simply joining tokens from complementary encoders works about as well as fancier fusion, and that encoder choice matters. Useful as the plain-concatenation baseline in the literature (Q4).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 6. Cambrian-1: A Fully Open, Vision-Centric Exploration of Multimodal LLMs

### 7. Theia: Distilling Diverse Vision Foundation Models for Robot Learning
- Authors: Shang et al. (2024)
- Venue: CoRL 2024 (as stated in the arXiv page comments)

- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q3 novelty vs prior selection work | Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q7 WAM background

### 8. PVI: Plug-in Visual Injection for Vision-Language-Action Models
- Authors: Zhang et al. (2026)
- Venue: arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed (venue not checked)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12772
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q7 WAM background
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It adds a second encoder (V-JEPA2 or DINOv2) to a robot policy through zero-initialized connections so the original behavior is not disturbed. That is a different way to combine encoders from our plain concatenation, so it is a useful contrast for Q4.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 9. Characterizing and overcoming the greedy nature of learning in multi-modal deep neural networks
- Authors: Wu et al. (2022)
- Venue: arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed (venue not checked)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05306
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q2 measuring what B adds to A
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It proposes a way to measure how much a model actually relies on each input and argues the model leans on one and underfits the other. A reliance measure like that could tell us whether the second encoder is being used at all (Q2).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 10. AM-RADIO: Agglomerative Vision Foundation Model -- Reduce All Domains Into One
- Authors: Ranzinger et al. (2023)
- Venue: CVPR 2024 (as stated in the arXiv API record)

- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches

### 11. RADIOv2.5: Improved Baselines for Agglomerative Vision Foundation Models

### 13. CoME-VL: Scaling Complementary Multi-Encoder Vision-Language Learning
- Authors: Deria et al. (2026)
- Venue: arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed (venue not checked)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03231
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q6 middle-layer features
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It fuses a SigLIP-style encoder with DINO using layers from several depths, projections meant to reduce redundancy between the two, and attention to line up the two feature grids. Those are exactly the fusion-step pieces we suspect (redundancy, grid matching, layer choice).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 14. Balanced Multimodal Learning via On-the-fly Gradient Modulation
- Authors: Peng et al. (2022)
- Venue: CVPR 2022, Oral (as stated in the arXiv page comments)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15332
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q4 fusion-step suspects
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: The abstract says that when one input dominates, a single shared objective leaves the other input under-trained, and it fixes this by adjusting each input's training strength. A possible remedy to test if one encoder is crowding out the other.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)
