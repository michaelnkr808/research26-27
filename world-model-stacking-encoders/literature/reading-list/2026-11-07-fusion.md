# Reading list: fusion (combining several vision encoders, and why adding an input can hurt)

This theme covers two things. First, papers that put several vision encoders into one model (multimodal language models, robot policies, and "multi-teacher" distillation that squeezes several encoders into one). Second, papers about why giving a jointly trained model an extra input or an extra loss term can make it worse, which is the same shape of puzzle as DINOv3 + C-RADIOv4 doing worse than either alone.

Date: 2026-11-07. Leads only, except entry 1 (read by the user, 2026-11-07); nothing here is cited yet (_standards/02). Every entry below was found by a search this session, and its arXiv page (or arXiv API record) was opened to check the title. Relevance notes come from the abstract unless an entry says it was checked in the paper body. Venues are given only where a page I opened states one, and each entry says which page.

Touches key: Q1 why a pair of encoders can hurt; Q2 measuring what encoder B adds to A; Q3 what is new vs prior model-selection work; Q4 the fusion-step suspects (feature scale, near-duplicate targets, grid resizing); Q5 the perturbed-frame test; Q6 middle-layer features; Q7 background on world action models.

Start here: (1) C-RADIOv4 (Tech Report), (2) ViTok, (3) What Makes Training Multi-Modal Classification Networks Hard?

### 1. C-RADIOv4 (Tech Report)
- Authors: Ranzinger et al. (2026)
- Venue: arXiv preprint; no venue on the arXiv record
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17237
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: This is the C-RADIOv4 tech report itself. Its abstract says the model is distilled from several teachers and the page names SigLIP2, DINOv3 and SAM3 as teachers, which is the overlap with DINOv3 that could explain why that pair hurts (Q1).
- Read status: read in full by the user, 2026-11-07 (bib key ranzinger2026cradiov4)

### 2. ViTok: Improving Dense Semantics in AM-RADIO-Style Multi-Teacher Distillation with PHI-S and Masked Image Modelling
- Authors: Xu et al. (2026)
- Venue: arXiv preprint; no venue on the arXiv record
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02903
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q4 fusion-step suspects
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: The abstract says "naively adding extra teachers such as SAM3 or HOG features introduces interference," and also reports that changes helping one task can hurt another, plus teacher reweighting and PHI-S feature balancing. This is the nearest published statement that adding a source can hurt through the combining step (Q1, Q4), though it is about distillation targets, not stacked inputs.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 3. What Makes Training Multi-Modal Classification Networks Hard?
- Authors: Wang et al. (2019)
- Venue: CVPR 2020 (as stated in the comments line of the arXiv page)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12681
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q4 fusion-step suspects
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: The abstract says a model given several inputs often does worse than the best single-input model, and blames overfitting at different speeds per input. That is a concrete candidate reason a second encoder can hurt (Q1).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 4. Encoder Winners Do Not Reliably Transfer Across VLA Backbone Scale: A Frozen-Backbone Grafting Diagnostic
- Authors: Zeng et al. (2026)
- Venue: arXiv preprint; no venue on the arXiv record
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14153
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q2 measuring what B adds to A | Q3 novelty vs prior selection work
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: Per the abstract it swaps single frozen encoders into robot policies (two LIBERO suites, two backbones) and scores them by offline action error, not closed-loop success, and finds the winner changes with backbone size. It does not test combinations of encoders, but it is a close neighbor on encoder selection and on how far a cheap test can be trusted (Q3).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 5. Modality Competition: What Makes Joint Training of Multi-modal Network Fail in Deep Learning? (Provably)
- Authors: Huang et al. (2022)
- Venue: ICML 2022 (Proceedings of the 39th ICML, as stated on https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/huang22e.html)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12221
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: The abstract proves, in a simplified late-fusion setting, that inputs compete and the network learns only some of them, which would explain a combined model losing to the best single one. Check how far it carries to our concatenation (Q1).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 6. PHI-S: Distribution Balancing for Label-Free Multi-Teacher Distillation
- Authors: Ranzinger et al. (2024)
- Venue: arXiv preprint; no venue on the arXiv record
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01680
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q1 why a pair hurts
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: It looks at how the scale and spread of each teacher's features, and the loss used, change the result when several teachers are matched at once. This is analogous to our suspects of feature scale and equal loss weights (Q4), but it normalizes teacher targets in distillation, not concatenated inputs.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 7. Eagle: Exploring The Design Space for Multimodal LLMs with Mixture of Encoders
- Authors: Shi et al. (2024)
- Venue: ICLR 2025 (as stated on the ICLR proceedings page, proceedings.iclr.cc, abstract page for this title)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15998
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q3 novelty vs prior selection work
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: The abstract reports that joining visual tokens from complementary encoders works about as well as fancier fusion, and that encoder choice matters. Useful as the plain-concatenation baseline in the literature (Q4).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 8. Theia: Distilling Diverse Vision Foundation Models for Robot Learning
- Authors: Shang et al. (2024)
- Venue: CoRL 2024 (as stated in the comments line of the arXiv page)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20179
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q3 novelty vs prior selection work | Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q7 WAM background
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: A robot-learning paper that merges several vision encoders into one by distillation. The abstract hypothesizes that higher entropy in the feature-norm distribution goes with better robot learning; that is about feature statistics, which is related to but not the same as feature scale (Q4).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 9. CoME-VL: Scaling Complementary Multi-Encoder Vision-Language Learning
- Authors: Deria et al. (2026)
- Venue: arXiv preprint; no venue on the arXiv record
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03231
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q6 middle-layer features
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: Per the abstract it fuses a contrastively trained vision encoder with a DINO encoder, merges layers from several depths, uses redundancy-reducing projections, and uses cross-attention to align the two feature grids. These touch on several of our fusion-step suspects (redundancy, grid matching, layer choice).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 10. DynaFLIP: Rethinking Robotics Perception via Tri-Modal-Dynamics Guided Representation
- Authors: Lee et al. (2026)
- Venue: arXiv preprint; no venue on the arXiv record (the paper text I read states none)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30350
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q7 WAM background
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: Checked in the paper body, Appendix D.1 (HTML version): DINOv2 is paired with CLIP, SigLIP or DynaFLIP, "concatenated at the feature level before being passed to the diffusion policy," with all encoders frozen on LIBERO. That is a frozen-pair concatenation setup like ours, in a policy rather than a world model. DynaFLIP is also one of the encoders in liu2026what (it is listed in the repo's liu2026what notes).
- Read status: Appendix D.1 and abstract read; rest not read

### 11. Characterizing and overcoming the greedy nature of learning in multi-modal deep neural networks
- Authors: Wu et al. (2022)
- Venue: ICML 2022 (Proceedings of the 39th ICML, as stated on https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/wu22d.html)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05306
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q2 measuring what B adds to A
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It proposes a way to measure how much a model relies on each input and argues the model leans on one and underfits the other. A reliance measure like that could tell us whether the second encoder is being used at all (Q2).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 12. PVI: Plug-in Visual Injection for Vision-Language-Action Models
- Authors: Zhang et al. (2026)
- Venue: arXiv preprint; no venue on the arXiv record
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12772
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q7 WAM background
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It adds a second encoder (V-JEPA2 or DINOv2) to a robot policy through zero-initialized connections so the original behavior is not disturbed. That is a different way to combine encoders from plain concatenation, a useful contrast for Q4.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 13. OpenVLA: An Open-Source Vision-Language-Action Model
- Authors: Kim et al. (2024)
- Venue: CoRL 2024 (Proceedings of the 8th Conference on Robot Learning, PMLR 270, as stated on https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/kim25c.html)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09246
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q4 fusion-step suspects | Q7 WAM background | Q3 novelty vs prior selection work
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: The abstract says its visual encoder combines DINOv2 and SigLIP features. Checked in the OpenVLA body (arXiv HTML, Section 3.1): "the resulting feature vectors are concatenated channel-wise," the same operation our stacking uses, so it is a direct robotics precedent for the combining step (Q4).
- Read status: abstract and Section 3.1 read; rest not read

### 14. Cambrian-1: A Fully Open, Vision-Centric Exploration of Multimodal LLMs
- Authors: Tong et al. (2024)
- Venue: NeurIPS 2024, Oral (as stated in the comments line of the arXiv page)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16860
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q3 novelty vs prior selection work | Q2 measuring what B adds to A
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It compares over 20 vision encoders by how well they work inside a language model, which is prior work on choosing encoders. ### 15. AM-RADIO: Agglomerative Vision Foundation Model -- Reduce All Domains Into One
- Authors: Ranzinger et al. (2023)
- Venue: CVPR 2024 (as stated in the arXiv API record and the arXiv page)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06709
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches

### 16. RADIOv2.5: Improved Baselines for Agglomerative Vision Foundation Models
- Authors: Heinrich et al. (2024)
- Venue: CVPR 2025 (as stated on the CVPR 2025 poster page, cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/poster/34144)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07679
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q4 fusion-step suspects
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: The abstract lists problems with multi-teacher distillation, including imbalance among teachers and shifts when input resolution changes. Those echo our worries about unequal loss weights and resizing grids (Q4). It is the predecessor of C-RADIOv4 (entry 1).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 17. Prismatic VLMs: Investigating the Design Space of Visually-Conditioned Language Models
- Authors: Karamcheti et al. (2024)
- Venue: ICML 2024 (as stated in the comments line of the arXiv page)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07865
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q3 novelty vs prior selection work | Q4 fusion-step suspects
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: The abstract is about design choices including which pretrained visual representation to use. Checked in the paper body, Section 4.2 "Ensembling Different Visual Representations" (arXiv HTML): "we simply concatenate patch features from different backbones along the channel dimension," and fusing DINOv2 and SigLIP is reported to give "significant gains across the board." That is the same concatenation operation as ours, in a language model. (I did not see OpenVLA mentioned in the part of the paper I read.)
- Read status: abstract and Section 4.2 read; rest not read

### 18. Balanced Multimodal Learning via On-the-fly Gradient Modulation
- Authors: Peng et al. (2022)
- Venue: CVPR 2022, Oral (as stated in the comments line of the arXiv page)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15332
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q4 fusion-step suspects
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: The abstract says that when one input dominates, a single shared objective leaves the other input under-trained, and it fixes this by adjusting each input's training strength. A possible remedy to test if one encoder crowds out the other.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)
