# Reading list: measures (comparing representations, and choosing models or combinations without training)

This theme covers two things. First, ways to compare what two networks' features contain, including what is wrong with CKA and measures of what one representation adds on top of another. Second, "transferability" work that scores pre-trained models, or sets of models, before any training happens.

Date: 2026-11-07. Leads only: nothing here is read or cited yet (_standards/02). Every entry was found by web search or the arXiv API in this session, and its link was opened (arXiv API record or venue page) with the title confirmed. Relevance notes come from the abstract only.

Start here: (1) Hewitt et al. 2021, conditional probing (#1); (2) Vimal K B et al. 2023, OSBORN ensemble selection (#2); (3) Guo et al. 2026, metric-guided fusion of visual foundation models (#3).

### 1. Conditional probing: measuring usable information beyond a baseline
- Authors: Hewitt et al. (2021)
- Venue: arXiv comment says "EMNLP 2021" (seen on the arXiv record; proceedings page not opened)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.09234
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q2 measuring what B adds to A | Q1 why a pair hurts
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: It asks how much a representation tells you that a baseline representation does not, which is our gain(B|A) idea in another field (language). It may already name, justify or warn about the exact quantity we computed.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 2. Building a Winning Team: Selecting Source Model Ensembles using a Submodular Transferability Estimation Approach
- Authors: Vimal K B et al. (2023)
- Venue: arXiv comment says "To appear at ICCV 2023"; the CVF open-access page listed it as ICCV 2023
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02429
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q3 novelty vs prior selection work | Q2 measuring what B adds to A
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: It is a direct follow-up to agostinelli2022 and scores a whole group of models, including how well the models in the group work together. We need to know how it differs from what we propose.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 3. Metric-Guided Feature Fusion of Visual Foundation Models for Segmentation Tasks
- Authors: Guo et al. (2026)
- Venue: arXiv comment says "Accepted to the CVPR 2026 Findings Track"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16864
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q3 novelty vs prior selection work | Q2 measuring what B adds to A
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: The abstract says naive fusion of foundation models (it names DINOv3 and SAM2) rarely gives reliable gains, and it proposes label-free feature metrics to pick what to combine. This is the closest problem statement to ours, though for segmentation not robot actions.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 4. Quantifying & Modeling Multimodal Interactions: An Information Decomposition Framework
- Authors: Liang et al. (2023)
- Venue: arXiv comment says "NeurIPS 2023"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12247
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q2 measuring what B adds to A | Q1 why a pair hurts
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: It splits what two inputs tell you about a target into shared (redundant), unique to each, and only-together (synergy) parts. A harmful pair might be nearly all redundancy, so this gives a vocabulary and an estimator for that idea. The abstract also mentions model selection and a robotic perception case study.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 5. Capturing Visual Environment Structure Correlates with Control Performance
- Authors: Dong et al. (2026)
- Venue: ICLR 2026 poster (stated on the iclr.cc poster page, opened 2026-11-07; the arXiv record has no venue note)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04880
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q3 novelty vs prior selection work | Q7 WAM background
- Relevance: high
- Why it might matter: It looks for a cheap probe of frozen vision encoders that predicts how well a robot policy does, so it is selection without rollouts for robotics. The abstract does not mention combining encoders, so our pair/combination angle may be the gap (to check when reading).
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 6. A Theory of Usable Information Under Computational Constraints
- Authors: Xu et al. (2020)
- Venue: arXiv comment says "ICLR 2020 (Talk)"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10689
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q2 measuring what B adds to A
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It defines information relative to a limited predictor, meaning only what that kind of model can actually use (a linear probe is our example, not the abstract's). The abstract says V-information includes R^2 (the coefficient of determination) as a special case, which fits our R^2-based gain, and conditional probing builds on this idea.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 7. Deconfounded Representation Similarity for Comparison of Neural Networks
- Authors: Cui et al. (2022)
- Venue: NeurIPS 2022 (stated on the neurips.cc poster page seen in search; arXiv record has no venue note)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00095
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q2 measuring what B adds to A
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It argues CKA is inflated by the shared structure of the input data and fixes it by regressing that structure out. That is a possible reason CKA missed our harmful pair, and "regress out A" is close in spirit to conditioning on A.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 8. One does not fit all! On the Complementarity of Vision Encoders for Vision and Language Tasks
- Authors: Geigle et al. (2022)
- Venue: arXiv comment says "Repl4NLP 2023" (a workshop)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06379
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q4 fusion-step suspects
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It tests whether giving a model features from several vision encoders helps, and looks at how the model fuses them. It is background for why some combinations help and others do not.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 9. What Representational Similarity Measures Imply about Decodable Information
- Authors: Harvey et al. (2024)
- Venue: UniReps 2024 workshop, PMLR volume 285 (stated on the proceedings.mlr.press page, opened 2026-11-07)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08197
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q2 measuring what B adds to A | Q1 why a pair hurts
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It shows that CKA, CCA and similar measures can be read as statements about how well linear decoders agree. This links our two tools (similarity and linear probes); our guess, not the abstract's claim, is that it may help explain why they disagree.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 10. Reliability of CKA as a Similarity Measure in Deep Learning
- Authors: Davari et al. (2022)
- Venue: ICLR 2023 poster (stated on the iclr.cc poster page, opened 2026-11-07; the arXiv record has no venue note)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.16156
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It studies how sensitive CKA is to changes that do not matter for function. It is evidence that CKA can come apart from what models actually do; one possible reason it missed our DINOv3 + C-RADIOv4 pair.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 11. Grounding Representation Similarity with Statistical Testing
- Authors: Ding et al. (2021)
- Venue: arXiv comment says "Accepted at NeurIPS 2021"

- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q1 why a pair hurts | Q2 measuring what B adds to A
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It gives a way to test whether a similarity measure responds to changes that matter for behavior. We could borrow that idea to check whether any candidate measure is worth trusting before using it.

### 12. LEEP: A New Measure to Evaluate Transferability of Learned Representations
- Authors: Nguyen et al. (2020)
- Venue: arXiv comment says "Published at the International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML) 2020"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12462
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q3 novelty vs prior selection work
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It is a basic "run the target data through the source model once and score it" measure. The ensemble-selection papers above (agostinelli2022 and OSBORN) are described as building on it, so it is the base of the prior work we must differ from. It needs a classifier's labels, which our robot setting lacks.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 13. LogME: Practical Assessment of Pre-trained Models for Transfer Learning
- Authors: You et al. (2021)
- Venue: arXiv comment says "ICML 2021 camera ready version"
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11005
- Link checked: 2026-11-07, title matches
- Touches: Q3 novelty vs prior selection work
- Relevance: medium
- Why it might matter: It is the standard feature-based way to rank single pre-trained models without fine-tuning, and it also handles regression, which is closer to predicting actions than classification-only metrics. A baseline for the "plain transferability" the mentor mentions.
- Read status: not read (abstract only)

### 14. Frustratingly Easy Transferability Estimation (TransRate)

### 15. Revisiting Model Stitching to Compare Neural Representations

- Venue: NeurIPS 2021 (stated on the proceedings.neurips.cc page, opened 2026-11-07; the arXiv record has no venue note)
