# What Visual Representations Do World Action Models Need? (Liu et al., 2026)
- **Link/DOI:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13024 (v1, 2026-10-09)
- **Tier:** 2 (preprint, not peer reviewed)
- **Read depth:** read by the user (confirmed 2026-10-16). The notes below were extracted by an agent on 2026-10-15; the numbers still need a check against the PDF before they go into a writeup.
- **Claim:** Adding frozen external visual features to a WAM (Wan 2.2-based) improves robustness. DINOv3 alone gives most of the gain, and further stacking adds little.
- **Method:** 13 frozen encoders (9 general-purpose, 4 specialized: SAM 3, VGGT-Ω, HRP, DynaFLIP). 5 fusion mechanisms; channel-wise concatenation is the default. Progressive composition (RQ3, App. E). Benchmarks: LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus (7 perturbation categories), RoboCasa GR1, RoboTwin 2.0, real Galbot G1.
- **Numbers as extracted (spot-check against the PDF):** LIBERO-Plus total: VAE-only 74.7, +DINOv3 84.0, R2-WAM (DINOv3+UniTok+V-JEPA 2.1) 87.3, +HRP 87.7. Adding C-RADIOv4 to DINOv3 gives 78.6 (hurts). Adding SAM 3 or VGGT-Ω to DINOv3 gives "little change". SigLIP 2 effects range from -0.4 to +1.8 depending on base. General-purpose avg 82.2 vs specialized 80.0.

- **How the paper benchmarks (verified, arXiv HTML v1 text, Sec. 4.1, 4.4, App. C.2, D.1, E.3; read 2026-10-27):**
  - Train once on LIBERO (Fast-WAM's processed data, all four suites, 1,712 episodes). Then the *same* checkpoint is scored three ways, with no retraining:
    1. **LIBERO (clean):** 50 rollouts per task × 40 tasks = 2,000 rollouts, averaged over the four suites. Near saturation (~97–99%), so only in App. D, Table 8.
    2. **LIBERO-Plus (LP):** 10,030 episodes per evaluation seed across 7 perturbation categories (camera, robot initial state, language, light, background, noise, layout), 3 evaluation seeds (41, 42, 43). The perturbation descriptions appended to LP instructions are removed (rewritten instructions kept in the language category). LP is the headline metric.
    3. Other benchmarks (RoboCasa GR1, RoboTwin 2.0, real Galbot G1) for the single-source study and the final recipe only, not the composition study.
  - **Composition (RQ3, Fig. 4, Table 10):** only the LP **total** per combo. No per-category LP numbers and no clean-LIBERO numbers for any combination, including DINOv3 + C-RADIOv4. One trained checkpoint per combo.
  - Single sources do get per-category LP (Table 1) and clean LIBERO (Table 8). C-RADIOv4 alone: LP 83.5 (DINOv3 84.0), clean LIBERO 98.2; per category camera 62.6, robot 81.0, language 92.2, light 97.3, background 86.2, noise 86.6, layout 83.6. So C-RADIOv4 alone is about as good as DINOv3 (better on background and noise); only the pair is bad.
  - Composition eval settings (E.3): 10 inference steps, replan every 10 steps, 30 initial waiting steps, no action ensembling, text guidance 1.0, binarized gripper.
  - **Implication for us:** our diagnostics never run a policy. They compare frozen-feature probe scores on clean libero_goal training demos (one suite of four) against the paper's LP totals. The harm we try to predict is measured under perturbations we never look at.

  - **Q1, how several encoders are run and combined (PLAN Next 3):**
    - Verdict on "features from different encoders are combined at matching locations": **supported for channel-wise concatenation, the paper's default and the only mechanism used for multi-encoder composition.** Sec. 3.3 (p5): "Features from each source are spatially aligned with the VAE latent grid and concatenated along the channel dimension." App. E.1 (p22), Eq. 8: f_t = Concat_ch{f_t^k : k in K}, then z~_0 = [z_0^vae ∥ f_0] and z~_{1:T,τ} = [z_{1:T,τ}^vae ∥ f_{1:T,τ}], "in a fixed source order". So every encoder is stacked on the same VAE latent positions, along channels, in the same vector as the VAE channels. App. B.2 (p15): "This preserves the video token grid and attention sequence length."
    - **What each encoder receives:** only "extract representations from the RGB observations available to the base WAM" (Sec. 3.2, p4), all encoders frozen. LIBERO: 2 RGB views, 224 × 224 per view (App. C.1, Table 7, p18); 9 video frames per view, sampling offsets 0, 4, ..., 32 (Table 6, p17). App. D.3 (p21) gives DINOv3's output on LIBERO: "Each view has a 14 × 14 grid of 1,024-dimensional features" (agent-view and wrist cameras). Not stated: whether the encoder input is exactly the 224 × 224 frame (or resized per encoder patch size), whether it sees all 9 frames or only some, whether it runs per frame or on a clip (relevant for video encoders such as V-JEPA 2.1, VideoMAE V2), and how the two views are laid out in the latent grid.
    - **Layer and tokens:** final layer, token features (App. A, p14: "We use token features from the final layer of each encoder"). Qwen3.5-4B: "final-layer hidden states at visual-token positions from a single forward pass". Checkpoints: "primarily large (L) variants", no names.
    - **Alignment to the latent grid:** the only text is "spatially align ... with the VAE latent grid" (Sec. 3.3 and Sec. 4 intro, p5; App. B.2, p15). The resize method for channel-wise concatenation is **not stated**. (The explicit "spatial resizing is bilinear" at App. B.2, p14, is for REPA's teacher target only.) The temporal match between the encoder's frames and the VAE's latent time steps (t = 0 plus T future steps) is also **not stated** for channel concat; it is stated only for REPA ("the observed temporal slice is preserved, and the eight future slices are averaged into two groups of four", p14).
    - **Projection or normalization before combining:** **not stated** for channel concat. The only stated change is that the video expert's "input and output projections are expanded to accommodate the added features while retaining the pretrained VAE weights" (App. B.2, p15): one widened input projection on the concatenated vector, and a widened output projection predicting the velocities. No per-encoder projector and no per-source normalization or standardization is described. LayerNorm before the projection is described only for token-wise concatenation (p15); gated cross-attention uses "normalized features" (p14). Encoders with different widths C_f simply add different numbers of channels. App. E.1 (p22) says "Source-specific processing and concatenation order are held fixed between training and evaluation" without saying what the processing is.
    - **Where features enter:** both. Observed (t = 0) features are clean inputs; future features are noised and predicted. App. B.2 (p15): "Observed features remain clean, whereas future VAE latents and external features are jointly noised and predicted. At inference, future features are initialized from noise and participate in joint denoising; only the VAE latents are decoded." Eq. 8 (p22): "The current state z~_0 remains clean." Each source has its own future-feature flow-matching loss, summed (Eq. 9). Current-only vs future-only vs both: Table 9, already above.
    - Caveat on "matching locations": the paper gives no grid size beyond DINOv3's 14 × 14, so how encoders with different native grids (patch sizes, video tubelets) are brought to one grid is a gap, not a verified step.
  - **Q2, which VLA (PLAN Next 5):**
    - **The paper does not use a VLA. Its model is a world action model (WAM).** Sec. 3.1 (p3-4): "the base WAM jointly predicts future-video latents z^vae_{1:T} and an action chunk a. The model comprises a video expert initialized from Wan 2.2 ... and a separate action expert." Language goes through T5 ("T5-encoded language features", p4). Three WAM formulations: joint denoising (default), direct action prediction (Fast-WAM style), video-then-action (IDM, LingBot-VA style) (App. B.1, p14).
    - The string "VLA" does not appear in the paper's body text (searched the full text file). Where VLAs do appear:
      - **Baselines in Table 3 (Sec. 4.5, p9):** (a) RoboCasa GR1: GR00T-N1.6, DiT4DiT, FLARE, LDA-1B, DIAL, WALA, LAWA; (b) RoboTwin 2.0: π0.5, Motus, StarVLA-α, InternVLA-A1, Fast-WAM, LingBot-VA, WALA-Wan. The text never calls any of them VLAs. Only reference titles do: π0.5 "A vision-language-action model with open-world generalization" (p10); StarVLA-α "...vision-language-action..." (p13); DIAL "end-to-end VLA" (p10). Which other rows are VLAs (GR00T-N1.6, InternVLA-A1) the paper does not say.
      - **The benchmark:** the LIBERO-Plus reference title is "A progressive robustness benchmark for visual-language-action models" (Fei et al., p10). The paper evaluates its own WAM on it, not a VLA.
      - **A language model inside the study:** Qwen3.5 (Qwen3.5-4B, "multimodal language modeling", Sec. 3.2 p4; App. A p14) is one of the 13 frozen external visual encoders (final-layer hidden states at visual-token positions). It is a feature source, not the policy. Result in Table 1 (p6): 81.6 total.
    - So if the mentor's question was "which VLA is the base model", the paper's answer is none: the base is a Wan 2.2 video expert plus an action expert. Which VLA the mentor had in mind the paper does not settle.
  - **Q3, integration mechanisms and fusion (for the later experiment design):**
    - **Five mechanisms (Sec. 3.3, p4-5; Fig. 2; App. B.2, p14-16; Table 4, p16):** REPA (alignment loss at the 8th video-expert block through a 3-layer MLP; no feature input at inference); gated cross-attention (action queries over current-frame features, per-layer zero-initialized tanh gate, no feature loss); channel-wise concatenation; token-wise concatenation (separate token sequence, shared video-expert layers); feature expert (its own 30-block transformer for future features). The paper calls them "feature integration" or "injection" mechanisms, not "fusion"; the word "fused" appears only for how feature and VAE representations are combined in the three joint-prediction mechanisms (p4).
    - **Tested with:** all five with each of the 13 single sources (Sec. 4.3, p7; per-source numbers in App. D.1 Table 8, p19-20). Average LP across the 13 (Fig. 3(a), p7): channel-wise concat 81.5 (+6.8), feature expert 76.4 (+1.7), REPA 76.2 (+1.5), gated cross-attention 74.7 (0.0), token-wise concat 72.7 (-2.0), against VAE-only 74.7. Text (p7): channel-wise concat "exceeding the next-best mechanism by 5.1 percentage points".
    - **Paper's conclusion:** Takeaway 2 (p8): "Use channel-wise concatenation as the default. For new designs, support future-feature prediction and encourage the action expert to make effective use of the added features." Reasons offered: future-feature prediction (Table 9) and higher action sensitivity to the features (Fig. 3(d), App. D.3). Fig. 3(c) text (p7): token-wise predictions "also retain recognizable structure", the feature-expert prediction "is coarser in the displayed example".
    - **Was any other mechanism tried on a multi-encoder composition? No.** Sec. 3.3 (p5): "We evaluate feature composition using channel-wise concatenation as the common integration mechanism." Sec. 4.4 (p8): "keep channel-wise concatenation fixed". So DINOv3 + C-RADIOv4 was run only with channel-wise concatenation (Fig. 4; Table 10, p23: D+R 78.59 ± 1.01, seeds 41/42/43 = 79.74, 77.82, 78.23). Whether another mechanism avoids the D+R drop is not tested.
    - **Per-source normalization / "source-specific processing":** not described anywhere (App. E.1, p22 names it only; see Q1). No per-source scale, whitening, or standardization is stated. Searched Sec. 3, App. A, B.2, E.
    - **Loss weights:** Eq. 9 sums L_FM(f^k_{1:T}) over sources; "Unless otherwise specified, auxiliary feature-prediction losses use a weight of 1.0" (App. B.2, p16), with L = L_WAM + λ_aux L_aux (Eq. 2, p5). The feature expert and REPA use 0.1 (p16). No per-source weights are tuned or compared, so in D+R both encoders' feature losses carry weight 1.0. Current features f0 are "excluded from the loss" (p22). How much each source's channel count shifts its pull on the shared output is not discussed.
    - **Why some compositions hurt (the paper's explanations):** Sec. 4.4 (p8): "The composition results suggest that overlap in pretraining objectives can limit complementary gains." Specifics: SigLIP 2 and UniTok both learn language-aligned representations; SigLIP 2 adds 1.8 to DINOv3, reduces success by 0.4 once UniTok is included, adds 0.2 when V-JEPA 2.1 is also present. VGGT-Ω "uses a DINOv3-initialized visual encoder", and adding it or SAM 3 to DINOv3 "produces little change". "A direct teacher–student relationship occurs with C-RADIOv4, which distills DINOv3, SigLIP 2, and SAM 3: combining it with DINOv3 reduces LP success to 78.6%." Intro (p2) raises "redundant information or interference during optimization" as a possibility, but no experiment tests redundancy or interference (no similarity metric, no ablation). The explanations are qualitative; treat them as the authors' hypothesis.
    - Oddity: Table 5 (REPA sensitivity, p17) lists "CLIP" among five encoders, but CLIP is not among the 13 sources in Sec. 3.2 or Table 1. The paper does not explain this.

I found no experiment, table, or appendix that extracts, compares, or mixes intermediate layers of any frozen encoder. Searched for layer, intermediate, depth across Sec. 3-4 and App. A-E.
    - **WAM side, one layer study (REPA only):** the REPA student is "the output of the eighth video-expert block" (App. B.2, p14). Table 5 (p17) varies the **WAM** layer used for alignment, d = 8, 15, 22 at weight 0.1 (and weight 0.5 at d = 8), across five encoders (CLIP, DINOv3, SigLIP 2, VGGT-Ω, V-JEPA 2.1), LP success. Means 76.73 / 76.80 / 76.77 / 76.71; DINOv3 row 78.05 / 77.36 / 77.86 / 78.02. This varies which WAM depth is aligned to the encoder's final-layer features, not which encoder layer is used.
