## Summary

1. **Novelty (speculative):** The exact question (a cheap conditional measure of what B adds to A, on frozen robot-data features, predicting which encoder pairs help or hurt a world action model) was not found in prior work. But the gap is narrower than the 2026-10-19 novelty statement says. The closest work I found that the repo's novelty statement does not mention is **KAGES** (Liu and Jiang 2026, arXiv 2608.17490): training-free, incremental ("marginal gain") selection of frozen encoders for concatenation-style fusion, which shows that adding encoders can hurt and uses a CKA-diversity baseline. It is image classification with labels, not robots, and it does not test pair harm against closed-loop success. A second missing neighbour is **Beyond Encoder Accumulation** (Ding et al. 2026, arXiv 2606.03879), which retrains all 31 subsets of 5 encoders in a multi-encoder VLM and finds pairing the two strongest encoders suboptimal, but offers no pre-training predictor. Our gain(B|A) itself is close to conditional probing (Hewitt et al. 2021), so the measure is not new; the novelty has to rest on the setting (frozen encoders as inputs and targets in a WAM), on predicting harm, and on the closed-loop ground truth.
2. **Starting fact (verified):** liu2026what v1 (still the only version) says "Results use one trained checkpoint per configuration" (App. C.2) and Table 10's SD "reflects evaluation-seed variability for a fixed trained checkpoint" (caption), with seeds 41, 42, 43 (E.3). D+R = 79.74 / 77.82 / 78.23, 78.59 ± 1.01. "Source-specific processing" is named but not described (E.1). Our CSVs and the paper note agree with the HTML on every value I checked. I found no later work that reproduces, retracts or qualifies the D+R result (searches only; citation API blocked).
3. **Planned steps:** (a) perturbed-frame probe: no direct prior found; still worth doing. (b) seed-noise check: not answered for this setup; still essential. (c) fusion suspects: neighbours exist (PHI-S on feature scale, C-RADIOv4's own Sec. 2.5, modality-competition papers) but none tests a WAM. (d) intermediate layers: **partly answered already** for one encoder: LiLa-WAM (arXiv 2608.03701), a WAM on frozen DINOv3, reports a large gain from block 17 over the final block. (e) motion-alignment score: my own searches agree with the prior-art file (no spatial label-free "where do features change when the scene moves" score found; Tsagkas et al. is the baseline).
4. **Citation fact-check:** all 5 bib entries exist and match on title, authors, year and arXiv id. Two have published versions the bib does not record: LIBERO (NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks) and LIBERO-Plus (CVPR 2026, under a different title, which is the version liu2026what cites). The agostinelli2022 note checks out against the PDF text. The liu2026what note checks out on every point I tested; one cited claim (SAM 3 uses a Perception Encoder backbone) is still unverified.
5. **Promise (speculative):** If the proxy reproduces the D+R drop over training seeds and some cheap measure flags it, this is a plausible CoRL/RSS paper (more likely with more than 4-7 pairs). If the proxy does not reproduce it, the strongest outcome is a careful replication/negative note, workshop-level on its own. The single result that most changes the answer is whether the D vs D+R gap survives training-seed noise in the proxy (Next 1 and stage 4).

## 1 Novelty of the main question

Question (README line 5): can a cheap, conditional measure of what encoder B adds to encoder A, computed on frozen features from robot data, predict which encoder combinations improve a WAM before training, where CKA does not?

### Closest works found (ordered by closeness; all judgements speculative)

1. **KAGES: "When More Foundation Models Means Less: Diagnosing and Addressing Multi-View Fusion Failure"**, Liu and Jiang, arXiv 2608.17490 v1, 18 Aug 2026, cs.CV. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17490 (abstract page and HTML v1 opened; first 100k characters read, targeted questions only).
   - verified (HTML): greedy selection by marginal gain "ΔJ(m|S) = J(S∪{m}) − J(S)" (Sec. 3.1); "training-free but not label-free" (Sec. 3), using a label kernel; linear-kernel sum equals the kernel of concatenated features (Sec. 3.2, Eq. 7); main protocol is a "Concat+MLP fusion head"; Table 1 shows peak-then-decline curves, e.g. "CLIP alone already beats every multi-encoder prefix" on some datasets (Sec. 2.4); a "Diversity" baseline selects by CKA between encoders (Sec. 4.1). Tasks: CIFAR-100, Pets, DTD, Country211, GTSRB, retrieval, frozen-LLM probes. No robotics in the text read.
   - How it differs: classification labels, not continuous actions; a fused linear/MLP head, not a closed-loop policy; no future-feature targets; no test of whether its score predicts a specific harmful pair. My fetch flagged a possible inconsistency between Fig. 1's caption and Table 1 (not checked further).
   - Why it matters: it already makes three of our claimed distinctions (conditional/incremental, frozen features, "adding encoders can hurt", CKA as baseline). The novelty statement must cite and differ from it.
2. **"Beyond Encoder Accumulation: Measuring Encoder Roles in Multi-Encoder VLMs"**, Ding, Zhang, Xie, Sun, Chen, Wang, arXiv 2606.03879 v1, 2 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03879 (abstract and HTML v1 opened).
   - verified (HTML): five encoders (ConvNeXt-1024, EVA-02-1024, CLIP-448, Pix2Struct-1024, SAM-1024), all 31 subsets retrained on the Cambrian-1 suite (Sec. 3.1); effective rank is computed on retrained checkpoints (Sec. 5); the heuristic is "not a derived selection rule for unseen pools" (Sec. 5.4); CKA used for drift analysis and a proxy PID (App. A.5-A.7). In Table 4 no combination falls below its best member (my fetch's reading).
   - How it differs: needs retraining every subset; VLM, not robot; no harmful pair. It is the "train everything, then explain" version of our question and a natural citation for "pairing the two strongest encoders is suboptimal".
3. **agostinelli2022** (already the repo's anchor). https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13011 (abstract page and v2 PDF text opened). verified: output averaging of separately fine-tuned members (Sec. 4.1: "we take the average of their predictions as the ensemble prediction"); Sec. 5 adds a light per-class weighting head before averaging; MS-LEEP reduces to a sum of single-model LEEP (Eq. 4). Differences in the note are correct (section 4 below).
4. **OSBORN: "Building a Winning Team"**, Vimal K B et al., ICCV 2023 per arXiv comment, https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02429 (abstract page only). verified (abstract): the ensemble score includes domain difference, task difference and "model-to-model cohesion". So a pairwise interaction term in ensemble transferability already exists; it is for classification/segmentation ensembles, and the abstract does not say whether fusion is output- or feature-level.
5. **Guo et al. 2026 (guo2026metric)**, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16864 (abstract page opened; the user has read it). Per-encoder label-free scores for fusion in segmentation; no pair-level score (per LOG 2026-11-07 [agent, literature], not rechecked by me).

Further neighbours (opened, abstract level): **Zeng and She 2026**, "Encoder Winners Do Not Reliably Transfer Across VLA Backbone Scale", https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14153: single-encoder grafting into VLAs on LIBERO, scored by offline action MSE, "not as a closed-loop deployment claim"; no combinations. **Dong et al. 2026**, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04880: frozen-encoder state probing correlates with policy success; single encoders. **Hurt 2026**, "Oracle Complementarity Is Not Realizable Complementarity in Frozen-Encoder Audio-Visual Emotion Recognition", https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31764: frozen encoders plus concatenation realize about half the oracle headroom on one dataset and near zero on another; a direct, if small, caution that a measured complementarity need not turn into fusion gain (audio-visual, not robots). **Hewitt et al. 2021**, conditional probing, https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.09234 (EMNLP 2021 per comment): "measure information in the representation that the baseline lacks", building on V-information; our gain(B|A) is a linear-probe instance of this idea (recalled that conditional V-information is defined as a difference of conditional V-entropies; the abstract does not give the formula). **Liang et al. 2023** (PID), https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12247, NeurIPS 2023: redundancy/uniqueness/synergy estimators, abstract claims use for "model selection" incl. a robotic perception case study.

Robot multi-encoder practice: the reading list's fusion entries 10, 13 and 17 record (checked by the literature session's reviewer, not reopened by me) channel-wise concatenation of DINOv2 with a language-aligned encoder in robot policies and VLMs. I found no 2025-2026 VLA or WAM paper other than liu2026what that reports a harmful frozen-encoder pair, and none that predicts pair effects before training (speculative; absence of evidence from 6 targeted searches).

### Where the gap is (speculative)

What no work found does at once: (i) frozen vision foundation encoders; (ii) pair-level, conditional and asymmetric score; (iii) robot data with continuous actions and future-feature targets; (iv) validated against closed-loop success with training-seed replicates; (v) explains a pair that is worse than both members. KAGES covers (i)-(ii) partially for classification; Dong, Burns and Tsagkas cover (iii)-(iv) for single encoders; liu2026what reports (v) without a measure. The defensible novelty is the combination, and specifically predicting harm in a WAM. Note that our own results so far say the simplest conditional measure does not flag D+R (README Key findings), so as of today the paper's constructive claim (H-B) has negative evidence; the gap is real but our current method does not fill it.

- `writing/hypotheses-draft.md` lines 55-61 (novelty statement, 2026-10-19): compares only with agostinelli2022. It does not mention KAGES (incremental selection on frozen encoders, harm from adding encoders), OSBORN (cohesion term), Ding 2026, or conditional probing. Its distinguishing claims "combining encoders can hurt" and "the encoders share no label space" are true against LEEP-family metrics but not against KAGES or LogME-style feature metrics, which work on frozen features with any labels. It also still says "We test whether a cheap conditional measure predicts closed-loop gains", which the tally has since answered "no" for the simplest version.

## 3 Prior work per planned step

All "worth doing" judgements are speculative.

### (a) Perturbed-frame probe test (PLAN Now, second bullet)
- Closest prior: **Burns et al.**, https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12444 (abstract opened): among ViTs, emergent segmentation predicts manipulation success under lighting, texture and distractor shifts better than ImageNet accuracy. **Dong et al. 2026** (above): frozen state-probe accuracy correlates with policy success. **LIBERO-Plus** (CVPR 2026 poster page opened, https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/poster/38735) is itself the per-factor analysis for VLAs (its per-factor findings were seen only in search summaries, not checked by me). **Tsagkas et al.** (HTML opened, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03270) report that per-patch features made a policy robust to distractors and lighting where a CLS-token policy failed (search summary of the paper; the passage was not opened by me).
- Already answered? No. None trains a probe on clean frames and tests it on matched perturbed re-renders, and none does it per encoder pair.
- Worth doing: yes, it is cheap and directly targets the mismatch between our clean-frame tests and the paper's perturbed benchmark. Caveat already in PLAN: 4 pairs makes any ordering match suggestive only.

### (b) Seed-noise check before the replication (PLAN Next 1)
- Closest prior: **Agarwal et al. 2021**, "Deep RL at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice", NeurIPS 2021 outstanding paper per comment, https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264 (few-run results need interval estimates). **Snyder et al. 2025**, RSS 2025 per comment, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10966 (sequential tests for comparing robot policies; the abstract covers evaluation trials only, not training seeds). **LeRobot LIBERO docs** (opened 2026-11-08), https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/en/libero: the reported 95% Wilson interval "covers which episodes were drawn; it does not cover the training seed of the policy, which needs more than one training run to measure."
- Already answered? Not for this setup. No source gives training-seed variance of a WAM on full LIBERO-Plus.
- Worth doing: yes, it is the precondition for everything else (section 5). It costs no extra runs.

### (c) Fusion-step suspects (PLAN Next 2)
- Scale: **PHI-S**, Ranzinger et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01680 (abstract): teachers' activation statistics differ; standardizing each dimension "to the same scale" gave the best student. C-RADIOv4 Sec. 2.5 (per LOG 2026-11-07 [agent, literature], not reopened): "Left unmitigated, DINOv3 would dominate the loss term". Both are distillation, not concatenated WAM inputs.
- Double counting / one input dominating: **Wang, Tran, Feiszli**, "What Makes Training Multi-Modal Classification Networks Hard?", CVPR 2020 per comment, https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12681 (abstract): multi-modal nets underperform the best single modality because modalities overfit at different rates. KAGES (above) shows the same peak-then-decline for frozen encoders.
- Grid mismatch: no direct prior found. A search summary attributes the point that interpolating encoder grids can lose information to reading-list fusion entry 14 (not opened by me, so not checked).
- Already answered? No; nothing tests these in a WAM with features as both inputs and targets.
- Worth doing: yes, under the PLAN's condition. Open question (b) in the hypotheses draft (suspect 2 may not be testable by undoing our choices) still stands.

### (d) Intermediate-layer features (PLAN Next 3)
- Closest prior: **LiLa-WAM**, Yang et al., arXiv 2608.03701 v1, 4 Aug 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03701 (HTML v1 opened). verified: "LiLa-WAM builds on a frozen DINOv3-ViT-L/16 encoder", "using features from its 17th and 21st blocks" (Sec. 4.1); Table 5 (10 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks): final block 48.0%, block 23 53.2%, block 21 57.4%, block 17 67.2%, blocks 17+21 70.0% (Sec. 4.3). **Perception Encoder**, Bolya et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13181 (abstract): the best embeddings sit in intermediate layers. Reading-list wam-context entries 9, 10 and 12 are further leads on intermediate layers (not opened by me).
- Already answered? Partly. For a single frozen DINOv3 in a WAM, "earlier layers help" is already reported (one paper, 10 tasks, single seed not checked). Not answered: whether layer choice changes pair effects (does D+R harm shrink with intermediate layers, or does R's overlap with D sit in late layers?).
- Worth doing: yes, but reframe toward the pair question so it is not a repeat of LiLa-WAM; and LiLa-WAM's block-17 result is a concrete starting point for which layers to extract.

### (e) Motion-alignment score (PLAN Next 4)
- My own searches (two queries, extended mode) found no label-free score of whether frozen features change where the scene moves. Search results pointed only to warp-error consistency metrics for output frames (not opened, so not named here) and to FlowFeat, which trains toward flow (prior-art file entry 13). This agrees with `writing/2026-11-08-motion-score-prior-art.md` verdict (a).
- Tsagkas et al. (HTML v3 opened, Sec. IV-B and Fig. 4): verified that the statistic is a whole-frame "average sequential cosine similarity" on task-centred features with no spatial or flow component. The Fig. 4 caption says "we omit here the non-temporal ResNets sub-group, as no trend emerged", while Sec. IV-B says the correlation is strong "within particular PVR subgroups, such as ResNets". These may refer to different ResNet groups; the user should read both when using this paper as the baseline (the prior-art file quotes only the caption).
- **4D-WAM**, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08023 (abstract): trajectory fields used as a training objective for WAMs, consistent with the prior-art file (not an encoder score).
- Already answered? No for the spatial version. Worth doing? Uncertain: it is a per-encoder score, while the main question is pair-level, and our only pair-level labels are 7 Table 10 pairs. Lower priority than (a)-(d) unless it yields a pair rule.

## 5 Promise verdict

All speculative.

- **If the proxy replication reproduces the D+R drop with 3 training seeds** and calibration passes: the paper has a verified harmful pair in an open, reproducible proxy, which liu2026what lacks (one checkpoint, no code). If one of the follow-up diagnostics (perturbed-frame probe, fusion suspects, layers) then explains or predicts the drop, a CoRL or RSS submission is plausible; ICLR/NeurIPS reviewers would ask for more pairs than 4-7 and for comparison with KAGES/OSBORN-style selection. If no cheap measure predicts it, the work becomes "why a stacked pair hurts", a mechanism paper, still plausible for CoRL/RSS if the mechanism is clean (for example, the drop disappears when one fusion choice changes).
- **If the proxy does not reproduce the drop** (or seed noise is as large as the effect): the main premise weakens. The likely output is a replication note ("a single-checkpoint harm does not survive training seeds" or "the proxy cannot see it"), workshop-level on its own, plus whatever the layer or motion threads yield as separate, smaller contributions. The fusion-suspects test is the main route back to a full paper in this branch.
- **Single result that most changes the answer:** the training-seed spread of D in the proxy compared with the D vs D+R gap (PLAN Next 1, then stage 4). Small spread plus a reproduced gap makes everything downstream meaningful; a large spread means even 3 seeds may not settle the starting fact, and the direction would need to change before the other 13 runs.
- Field timing: WAM papers built on frozen encoder features keep appearing (LiLa-WAM Aug 2026, 4D-WAM Aug 2026, OpenWAM Sep 2026, all opened above), and KAGES (Aug 2026) is already on the "more encoders can hurt" theme. The window for the pair-harm angle is open but narrowing.
