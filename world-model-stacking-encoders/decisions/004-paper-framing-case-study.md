- **Options considered:**
  - **A. Case study only.** The paper is about whether one reported harmful pairing is real and where it shows up. Deliverable by the running work. Drops the original idea entirely.
  - **B. Predictor paper.** Needs many multi-seed pair labels (each pair costs several training runs), or per-encoder scoring against Table 1's 13 single encoders. The second route drops the conditional angle and is close to Yardi et al. 2025's action-score probe. Not deliverable from the five configs we are running.
  - **C. A now, with B as the stated follow-up.** The paper is the case study; the conditional pair-score predictor is named as the follow-up, taken up only once there is a confirmed effect to predict.
- **Decision & rationale:** C. Question (README): when frozen encoders are stacked as inputs and future-feature targets of a world action model, is the reported harm from pairing DINOv3 with C-RADIOv4 (liu2026what Table 10) reproducible across training seeds, and does it leave any trace in frozen features (clean, perturbed, or as co-targets), or does it arise only in training? Rationale: the running experiments answer this question and no other; a predictor needs an effect to predict, and none is confirmed yet. Keeping B as a follow-up keeps the original idea alive without promising it.
