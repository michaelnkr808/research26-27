## Summary

In what we read (ST-AudioQA, RMS-AQA, SpatialSoundQA and HLR-AVSceneQA in full; ST-OmniQA and Sonic-Bench in their benchmark sections; others by abstract or grep), no audio-only spatial question answering benchmark asks about a source that stops and returns, two sources whose paths cross, or several same-class sources, with per-source identity as ground truth. Closest: ST-OmniQA (audio-visual, 10 s) has same-class sources and tracking after visual occlusion, scored by LLM-judged accuracy; SonicWorld / Sonic-Bench (arXiv 2610.17131, 10 s first-order ambisonics, a male and a female voice) checks tracking across a speech pause only as a two-clip appendix diagnostic; ST-AudioQA lists same-class ambiguity, source entrances and exits, and pauses as future work. All audio-only benchmarks read use clips of at most 10 s (RMS-AQA cuts its 60 s scenes into 10 s clips); the audio-visual SAVVY-Bench uses 30 to 300 s videos. No spatial question answering benchmark we read scores identity switches, IDF1 or HOTA; the only identity-style number found in a spatial question answering paper is SonicWorld's "ID retention" after a speech pause (App. E.3, Table E.1), reported for two example clips on the model's source states, not as a question answering score. Those metrics otherwise live in the multi-object tracking and audio source tracking literature, where Iatariene et al. (arXiv 2506.11145) adapt HOTA to intermittent, moving speakers, the closest match to the stop-and-return scenario. Four models with weights and inference code are usable as baselines today (MiDashengLM-Spatial, BAT, ST-AudioLM, Spatial-Omni), all short-clip: the longest spatial input is 60 s (MiDashengLM-Spatial, binaural), so a long recording needs chunking or a memory the model does not have; Audio Flamingo 3 takes 10 min but one channel only, so it can serve only as a no-spatial-cues control. Hear You Are and HLR-LLM are paper only as far as we could find. Moving sources are made in prior datasets by switching or crossfading per-position impulse responses along a trajectory: time-variant convolution in DCASE 2020 and SpatialScaper, and the same idea with SoundSpaces 2.0 in ST-AudioQA, ST-OmniQA and Sonic-Bench (reviewer correction: an earlier draft said SoundSpaces sources are static, true only of BAT and HLR-AVSceneQA). "World audio model" is not an established term; the closest uses are "audio world models" (arXiv 2512.08405) and "acoustic world models" (arXiv 2506.13833), each meaning something different.

ST-AudioLM was read in full by the reviewer (paper with appendices, README, data doc). Its benchmark, ST-AudioQA, has five primitive question families (event semantics, movement status, spatial location, spatial change, source grounding) and three compositional ones (temporal relation change, movement-conditioned spatial relation, cross-source trajectory relation), 10 s first-order ambisonics clips at 32 kHz with one or two sources, in static or dynamic combinations (S, D, SS, SD, DS, DD). Earlier drafts said "crossing-style pairs" and "the only model built for moving sources"; both were wrong or too strong (reviewer). Other moving-source models exist (Sridhar et al. arXiv 2602.16334, SonicWorld arXiv 2610.17131, ST-Omni-R1), but only ST-AudioLM among them has weights found. Binaural models and FOA models need different renders of the same scene, which matters for the sound-engine choice in the 2026-11-24 note.

## E. How prior datasets make moving and starting-and-stopping sources

- DCASE 2020 data (Politis et al., arXiv 2006.01919, Sec 2.4, `verified`): room impulse responses measured by recording a slowly moving loudspeaker along circular or linear tracks, extracted about every 200 ms and re-aligned to about 1-degree steps; moving events made by time-variant convolution (the event's STFT convolved with the impulse response of the current position); speed set by how many consecutive impulse responses are used per second (10, 20 or 40 degrees per second); each 1-minute mixture has random onsets, polyphony 1 or 2, and each event randomly static or moving.
- SpatialScaper (arXiv 2401.12238, text and `core.py` by grep): scaper plus pyroomacoustics (image source method) for synthetic rooms, or measured impulse response databases; each event has a start and end position, an onset time, an SNR and optional pitch and time-stretch; default movement is linear between the two points; `define_trajectory` has a `speed_limit` (default 1.5) and circular and linear shapes; in a measured room the path snaps to the nearest trajectory the recorded layout allows (Sec 3.3). How the impulse response is switched along the path is not described in the paper or the lines grepped (`recalled`: probably via the imported `traj_2_ir_idx`). Backgrounds loop for the whole soundscape.
- RMS-AQA reuses SpatialScaper for its simulated track and then cuts 60 s scenes into 10 s clips, which loses long-range tracking.
- SoundSpaces 2.0 as used by BAT and HLR-AVSceneQA: one impulse response per source and receiver pose, each source static, always on for the full clip. Reviewer correction: ST-AudioQA, ST-OmniQA and Sonic-Bench do make moving sources with SoundSpaces 2.0 or Habitat-Sim's acoustic engine by rendering one impulse response per time bin or step along the trajectory (ST-AudioQA: 0.25 s bins, 50 ms crossfade; Sonic-Bench: Habitat-Sim impulse responses along each trajectory, Sec 3.2).
- Not found in what was read: gpuRIR, and any generator that makes "stops, then returns" or crossing paths with identity labels for audio-only question answering (Sonic-Bench's speech-pause diagnostic is the nearest).

## C. Identity switches, re-identification and localization error

Read depth: MOT16, IDF1, HOTA, Politis 2020, LOCATA, Iatariene, Adavanne in full text or the relevant sections (pdftotext); DCASE 2025 and 2026 from task web pages or snippets (marked).

Multi-object tracking metrics (what each needs: ground-truth identities per frame or trajectory, plus a matching rule; in audio the matching threshold is an angle or a distance):
- ID switch: MOT16 (arXiv 1603.00831, lines 569 to 581 of the text). Counted when a ground-truth target is matched to a track and its last assignment was a different track; stricter than the original definition and best read relative to targets recovered. MOTA (MOT16 line 622; original Bernardin and Stiefelhagen 2008, not on arXiv, not read): 1 minus (misses + false positives + ID switches) over total ground-truth objects; can be negative.
- IDF1: Ristani et al., arXiv 1609.01775 (Eq. 11): one global assignment of whole predicted trajectories to whole ground-truth trajectories; harmonic mean of identification precision and recall; rewards keeping one ID for as long as possible.
- HOTA: Luiten et al., arXiv 2009.07736 (Eqs. 13 to 21): geometric mean of detection accuracy and association accuracy over localisation thresholds; association asks how well a detection's predicted and ground-truth tracks agree over all time.
- `speculative`: HOTA and IDF1 suit "source returns after a gap" and "crossing paths"; ID switches alone are weaker for "several same-class sources", because a swap that is later repaired is penalised only at the swap.

DCASE sound event localization and detection:
- Location-dependent F-score and error rate (Politis 2020, Sec 4.2): a prediction is a true positive only if the class matches and the angular distance is under 20 degrees, in 1 s segments. Class-dependent localization error (mean angle between same-class prediction and reference pairs) and localization recall (share of reference instances recalled), no distance threshold. Original: Mesaros et al., WASPAA 2019, not on arXiv, not read (`recalled`).
- DCASE 2024 added distance (20-degree location-dependent F, class-dependent direction error, relative distance error): from a search summary only, no page opened. DCASE 2025 stereo task (page, summarised): Hungarian matching on azimuth error, 20-degree threshold, relative distance threshold 1, 100 ms frames, macro F1; direction and distance errors unthresholded.
- Source identity: the 2025 labels carry a source index "provided only as additional information", not required and not scored. So SELD scoring never checks that "source 3 at time 10 is source 3 at time 40". DCASE 2026 Task 3 (page read by the reviewer): annotations carry an `instance_id` "to track sound sources across multiple frames", but the submission format has none and scoring is mask mAP plus Pearson r with per-frame, per-class Hungarian matching, so identity is not scored.

Audio source tracking:
- LOCATA challenge (Evers et al., arXiv 1909.01008): one-to-one source-to-estimate association after gating; accuracy, completeness, continuity, timeliness; Track Fragmentation Rate counts swaps plus broken tracks; OSPA cannot see identity swaps. Ground truth: per-source direction trajectories over active periods.
- Iatariene, Guérin, Serizel, "Tracking of Intermittent and Moving Speakers: Dataset and Metrics" (arXiv 2506.11145, sections 3 to 5): dataset LibriJump has speakers that move while silent, so tracks are discontinuous (the stop-and-return case). HOTA association metrics (AssA, AssPr, AssRe) adapted with angular distance replacing IoU, plus LOCATA's Track Swap Rate and Track Fragmentation Rate; they argue frame-level ID switches can penalise a tracker that corrects itself later. Ground truth: speaker identity and direction per frame.
- Adavanne, Politis, Virtanen (arXiv 2111.00030): sound event localization and detection tracking framed as MOTA (with identity switches) plus MOTP (equal to localization error); identity switches by comparing association matrices between consecutive frames, which needs source IDs.
- Ban et al., audio-visual speaker tracking (arXiv 1809.10961): reports MOTA, false positives and negatives, identity switches on AV16.3 and AVDIAR; only the first page read, and the id was not checked on the arXiv API.

Do spatial-audio question answering papers report anything like these? Checked by downloading each PDF and grepping for identity and tracking terms (`verified` for the grep; depth varies):
- BAT, RMS-AQA, SPUR (arXiv 2511.06606), Audio Flamingo 3, HLR-AVSceneQA, JAEGER, Sudarsanam and Politis (arXiv 2507.09195), DeepASA: no identity or tracking metrics (accuracy, mAP, direction and distance error, two-stage accuracy, LLM-judge scores, SELD metrics).
- SonicWorld (arXiv 2610.17131): reports "ID retention" (percent of frames after speech resumes in which the target stays uniquely matched to its pre-pause slot, slot assignments fixed), App E.3 Table E.1; the one identity-style number found in a spatial question answering paper (reviewer, appendix read).
- ST-AudioLM (Table 3): a "Tracking" column that is trajectory accuracy within 20 degrees on single-source moving clips (path, not identity). ST-OmniQA (arXiv 2608.09435): calls dynamic sound understanding "a tracking and binding problem"; accuracy only. Spatial Audio Motion Understanding and Reasoning (arXiv 2509.14666): multiple choice accuracy, about 30 percent on movement reasoning. The World is Not Mono (arXiv 2601.02954): accuracy on source-location binding; its encoder is scored with Hungarian matching on one-second segments; grep only.
- `speculative`: an identity metric for a question answering model would have to be built by us, for example "which source is which" questions scored per source, with per-source IDs and positions over time as ground truth.

## D. Is "world audio model" an established term?

Short check by the metrics worker: arXiv API queries for "world audio model", "audio world model", "acoustic world model" returned very few hits; the web search added a few. Titles and authors were taken from abstract pages (summarised, not full text).
- "Audio world models" in a title: Zhang and Gienger, arXiv 2512.08405, a generative latent flow-matching model that predicts future audio, used for robot manipulation; no formal definition on the page.
- "Acoustic world models": survey, arXiv 2506.13833 (Chen et al.), in the abstract; world models built on acoustic signals; no formal definition.
- Audio-Visual World Models (arXiv 2512.00883): action-conditioned audio and video simulator; uses "audio-visual world model", not the three phrases.
- AudioWorldSim (arXiv 2608.21075) and BinauralVAE (arXiv 2609.06837), Zerkowski and Velho: dataset and reconstruction work "for world models"; BinauralVAE names an audio-based world model as a goal; ids from search results, not checked on the API. "Audible World Models" (arXiv 2609.38444, Chen et al.; same caveat): training-free spatial sound for generated 3D worlds.
- "World Audio Model: A Unified Framework for Dynamic Acoustic World Representation, Predictive Simulation, Causal Reasoning, and Autonomous Learning" (ResearchGate 415545391): the only hit using "World Audio Model" as a named concept; ResearchGate returned 403, so only a search snippet was seen; not on arXiv in the queries; authors, date, venue, peer-review status unknown. A lead, not citable.
- Answer: not an established term; usage is split across predicting future audio, action-conditioned binaural simulation and sound generation for 3D worlds.
