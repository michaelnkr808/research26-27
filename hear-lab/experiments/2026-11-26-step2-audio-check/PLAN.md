# Plan: Step 2, make the sound and check it

Written and committed before running. Once the experiment starts, don't edit this design: add changes below as dated amendments.

Design approved by the lead in chat on 2026-11-26 (relayed to the worker that wrote this folder). Revised before any run on 2026-11-26 for the PR #95 review (heading gate, renderer inputs, alignment threshold, per-frame ear height, frame wording, sound hashes); no result existed then. Items marked **PROPOSED** are the worker's proposals inside that design; they are flagged for the lead and must be confirmed (or changed) by a commit before the first run (`config.yaml` `direction_check.proposals_confirmed_by_lead`, which `render_audio.py` checks).

**Question:** can we put a ringing telephone at the target object of real GN-Bench train_seen episodes, render what a listener walking the episode's reference path hears (RLR engine, MIT KEMAR head), one sound step per video frame, and does the left/right cue in that sound point to the correct side? This is the check that the audio Steps 3 and 4 will be built on carries a usable direction cue in the right frame, and the run that gives the cost per episode for those steps.

## Design

### 1. Episodes
- Split: GN-Bench train_seen only. No test_unseen episode is used.
- Source table: the route A check 3 train_seen table (1000 seeded rows of 17,123, 100 scenes; local only, read-only, never copied into git; pinned by sha256 in `config.yaml`), visible flag at the 0.15 m depth tolerance (`visible_primary`).
- Excluded: the 39 possibly mirrored scenes, recomputed from the same folder's `scenes.jsonl` by the check 3 rule (a scene counts when a mirrored frame has the largest splat-inside total, ties to identity), as rung 1 did (`../2026-11-21-rung1-wnm3d-baseline/LOG.md`, first entry). Recount on 2026-11-26 (`src/pick_episodes.py`, we ran it): 41 of the 1000 rows are in those scenes (8 visible), leaving 343 visible and 616 not-visible eligible episodes. All 1000 rows have status ok.
- Frozen order (`episode_order.txt`, committed; ids only, `<scene>_<trajectory_id>` as GN0's `get_episode_id`): one `numpy.random.default_rng(20261126)` permutes the visible ids, then the not-visible ids (each in check 3 row order before the permutation); the two are interleaved visible, not visible, visible, ... until the visible list runs out: 686 ids, sha256 `953eb06c4e0585f1ba10e3ce6d0c2d221c4855c2dda566e80fa85f3fe9727bbf`. Position 2k (0-based) is a visible start, 2k + 1 a not-visible start, so every even-length prefix is balanced.
- First batch: the first 12 ids (6 visible, 6 not; 11 scenes).
- Full set: **PROPOSED** the first 100 ids (50 visible, 50 not; 65 of the 100 train_seen scenes of the table, none of the route A low wall-share scenes). Why 100: it reaches about two thirds of the scenes in the table, so the per-scene report says something about scene-to-scene variation, while staying a small multiple of the first batch, whose timing prices it. The order holds 686 ids, so a larger full set needs no new pick. The full set is a new run (`full-v1`) that includes the batch 1 episodes again; its verdict is computed on that run alone.
- Alignment episodes (not part of the bar): the first 2 episodes of the released renderer's own train_seen order (`render_dataset.sh --start-idx 0 --end-idx 2`), which route A check 2 rendered to video on 2026-11-20. They are exported again and compared with those videos: the frame counts must match, and **PROPOSED** the mean absolute pixel difference per frame (0 to 255 scale) must have a median of at most 2.0 and a maximum of at most 10.0 over the episode (`config.yaml` `poses.alignment`). Why: both videos come from the same code, settings and encoder, so a correct export should differ only by GPU rounding and encoder noise, while a pose off by a few centimetres or degrees moves the whole image by many levels; the numbers are a judgment, not measured. Whatever the gate says, the lead reads the pixel numbers before the audio step is run (`RUN.md` step 4).

### 2. Paths and steps
- The path of an episode is its reference trajectory as GN0 renders it: `gn0/collection/trajectory_video.py` `TrajectoryVideoCollector` at GN0 `c927781`, with the train_seen config's `EVAL.RENDERER` settings (10 fps, the line-of-sight smoothed raster path resampled every 0.05 m, heading from a 0.5 m lookahead, yaw smoothing window 9), the same code route A check 2 used for its 48 videos (`render_dataset.sh`).
- `src/export_poses.py` runs that released collector unchanged (subclassed only to record the poses it renders, in its own order) and writes the video and the per-frame poses. Audio step k uses the pose of video frame k: same poses, same order, one step per frame. Frame rate 10 fps, so one step is 0.1 s of sound (4410 samples at 44.1 kHz); a frame is 0.05 m of path, so the listener moves at 0.5 m/s. The script checks that the number of rendered frames equals the number of poses for every episode.
- Listener pose per step: the video camera's. GN-Bench places the camera at `transform_from_world_to_GS(position)` looking at `transform_from_world_to_GS(position + forward)` (`GNBenchSim._render_observations`, GN-Bench-Tools `0ce8367`); the export records, for every frame, the eye and look-at target that `render_camera_gsplat` actually receives (a pass-through wrapper on that call, `GN_Bench_simulator.py` lines 979 to 990), and the listener uses that eye and the horizontal part of (target - eye). The same pose computed from the collector's rotations (scipy x y z w quaternions, forward = local +x) is kept beside it, and the episode fails if the two differ by more than 1 mm. Heading gate: the episode also fails if, on any moving frame, the camera forward is more than 45 degrees from the direction of travel (towards the point 0.5 m further along the path, the collector's own lookahead; at the last frame, the last step). Not gated: the first 5 frames, whose yaw the collector blends with the episode's start heading on purpose (yaw window 9), and frames that do not move. The collector resamples the path every 0.05 m and drops repeated points, so it has no turning-in-place frames; a frame without motion is counted and left out of the gate (expected none). Receiver height rule: the ears are at the camera position, which GN-Bench puts at 1.3 m above the floor (`_load_initial_state`, `GN_Bench_simulator.py` line 780; the collector keeps the start z for every frame); the export and the audio script check 1.3 m on every frame. This differs from the SoundSpaces convention of 1.5 m; it is chosen so that sound and video share one viewpoint.

### 3. Frames
- All audio geometry is in the InteriorGS frame, the frame the splats, `structure.json` and `labels.json` share (right-handed, z up, metres: a viewer standing upright and looking along +y has +x on the right and -x on the left; looking along -y, +x is on the left). The dataset card's axis names ("x right, y back") are not used for anything; the side rule below is fixed by the look-at rule of GN-Bench's renderer instead (camera image right = forward x up), and a Mac test checks that a point on the audio's left projects onto the left half of the rendered image at 24 headings. The mesh is built in that frame directly (no world map), the source is in it, and the listener eye and forward come from GN-Bench's own world-to-GS transform, so the sound is placed exactly as the picture is, whatever the per-episode affine is.
- Left = up x forward; a source is on the listener's left when (source - eye) . left > 0. The RLR listener quaternion maps the library's default local axes (right +x, up +y, back +z) onto the listener's right, up (+z) and back (`hear-lab/src/poses.py` `listener_quat_from_forward`). Binaural channel 0 is the left ear (route A: with the KEMAR file a source at +90 degrees reached channel 0 first, 0.73 ms ahead).

### 4. Source and sound
- Source position: the centre of the target object's `labels.json` box (mean of its 8 corners), the NaVLA2 convention, not the GN-Matrix goal point. The target's own box is left out of the acoustic mesh (as route A check 1) so the source is not sealed inside it. Logged per episode, local only: the horizontal distance from the GN-Matrix goal point to the box (as check 3 computed it), and whether the source point lies inside any other box.
- Sound: the SoundSpaces telephone ring, `sounds/1s_all/telephone.wav` from `http://dl.fbaipublicfiles.com/SoundSpaces/sounds.tar.xz` (`soundspaces/README.md` line 14 at SoundSpaces `287184f`; "telephone" is the AudioGoal default sound, the `*_telephone` splits). The lead downloaded it on 2026-11-26: archive sha256 `84152771...d8f8` (671 MB; a plain tar despite the .xz name), `telephone.wav` sha256 `3522b2eb...e9bd`, 1 channel, 44.1 kHz, 32-bit float, 1.000 s (full hashes in `config.yaml`; `render_audio.py` checks the wav's hash on the machine). After the -14 dBFS normalisation its peak is about 1.24 (above full scale; harmless in 32-bit float, the clips' level after propagation is lower; computed on the Mac from the lead's copy).
- Converted to one channel (mean of channels if it has more), resampled to 44.1 kHz if it is at another rate (`scipy.signal.resample_poly`), and loudness-normalised: RMS over the whole clip set to -14 dBFS (NaVLA2's stated level; NaVLA2 does not say whether its -14 dBFS is RMS, peak or LUFS, so the measure here is our choice).
- Looped for the whole episode; it never stops, also not on arrival. The sound is taken as having played before the episode starts (the first step already has a full reverberant tail). Start offset: a uniform random sample within the first step, `default_rng(seed + order position)`, as SoundSpaces 2.0 draws `randint(sr * STEP_TIME)`.

### 5. Render
- Engine: RLR-Audio-Propagation `4fd446b` through `hear-lab/src/rlr_binding.py`; HRTF: MIT KEMAR normal pinna SOFA (`config.yaml` `rlr.hrtf`, sha256 `e7035994...`), loaded with `RLRA_SetListenerHRTF`.
- SoundSpaces 2.0 settings (`soundspaces/continuous_simulator.py` lines 115 to 126 at `287184f`): transmission on, `indirectRayCount` 500, materials off (every face gets the library's default material, as habitat-sim's AudioSensor does with `enableMaterials = False`; no material database), temporal coherence on (one context per episode, the same listener moved step by step), `threadCount` 1 (the same as SoundSpaces 2.0). Every other field stays at the library default and is logged. Differences from SoundSpaces 2.0: the HRTF (SoundSpaces 2.0 sets none, so it uses the library's built-in head model, which route A found to carry almost no time difference); the receiver height (1.3 m, above); the geometry (our floor-plan-and-boxes mesh, not a scanned mesh); the step length (0.1 s per video frame, not 0.25 s).
- Sample rate 44.1 kHz (the library default and the KEMAR file's own rate, so the HRTF is not resampled). Per-step two-ear room impulse responses are saved at that rate, whole (the general artifact; Step 3 chooses its window, Spatial-AST expects 10 s at 32 kHz).
- Listening clips per episode: the looped ring through the per-step responses, one 0.1 s step per video frame, the first 0.05 s of each step crossfaded linearly from the previous step's response (SoundSpaces 2.0 `crossfade()`, `int(0.05 * sr)` samples); two-ear, and one-channel = mean of left and right of the same render. Clip length = number of frames x 0.1 s, the video's length. 32-bit float WAV.
- Episodes run in parallel processes (`config.yaml` `run.workers`), each with `threadCount` 1.
- Timing: scene load (mesh build; context, mesh upload and HRTF), every step's simulate time, the per-step checks, the clips, and the per-episode total, plus the pose export's per-episode render time, so the first batch gives the cost per episode for Steps 3 and 4.

### 6. Direction check (the pass bar, fixed before any run)
- Per step, the interaural time difference (ITD) of the two-ear impulse response: onset = first sample where either ear reaches 0.1 x the larger ear peak; window = the same samples of both ears from 0.25 ms before to 2.0 ms after the onset; ITD = the lag (within +-1.0 ms) of the largest value of the cross-correlation c(l) = sum L[n] R[n + l] (signed maximum, not absolute); positive = the right ear later = the cue says left. The sign is compared with the true side of the source relative to the listener heading (section 3).
- Counted steps: (a) line of sight: the straight segment from the ears to the source, shortened by 1 cm at each end, hits no triangle of the acoustic mesh (`RLRA_TraceRayAnyHit` after the step's simulation; the target box is not in the mesh); and (b) the source clearly to one side: **PROPOSED** the lateral angle (angle between the direction to the source and the listener's median plane, asin of (v . left / |v|) with the full 3D direction v) is at least 20 degrees. In the horizontal plane this drops the front zone |azimuth| < 20 degrees and, by the same margin, the rear zone |azimuth| > 160 degrees; with the source above or below the ears it also drops steps close to the median plane in 3D, where the time cue is small. Why 20 degrees: a spherical head (Woodworth, radius 8.75 cm) gives about 0.18 ms at 20 degrees lateral (KEMAR measured a bit larger than Woodworth at 90 degrees in route A: 0.73 ms against 0.66 ms), about 8 samples at 44.1 kHz, well above the one-sample resolution, and the margin keeps out steps where a few degrees of heading smoothing (the renderer's yaw window and lookahead) could move the source across the median plane.
- Bar: at least 90 percent of the counted steps, pooled over the batch, have the correct sign. **PROPOSED** a minimum: if fewer than 200 steps are counted in the batch, the verdict is TOO_FEW (not a pass), and the batch goes back to the lead. A verdict is given only for the whole planned batch: if any of its episodes failed to render, the verdict is INCOMPLETE (Codex review finding, before any run). Also reported: per episode, per scene, visible against not-visible starts, the route A low wall-share scenes (0001_839920, 0756_840012) separately if any are in the set (none are in the first 100 of the order), Wilson 95 percent intervals, and the steps where the estimate finds no side (counted as wrong).
- Occluded steps (no line of sight): reported with their sign agreement, no bar.
- Front/back is not tested.
- One-channel: its interaural difference is zero by construction (both ears are the same signal); reported as such, not measured.
- The full set is held to the same bar and counting rules.
- Before any episode, a preflight on the rented machine with the real library and the KEMAR file (free field, direct sound only, source 2 m to the left and then to the right, headings 0, 90, 180 and 270 degrees in the InteriorGS frame) must give the right side in all 8 cases; otherwise no episode is rendered and the lead is told.

### 7. Pictures and clips for the lead's own judgment
Per episode, a top-down picture (occupancy map, the listener path, the source, and per step an arrow to the side the ITD says, coloured by whether it matches the true side or was not counted) and the two clips. They are InteriorGS-derived: local only, never in git. The agent gives no verdict on them.

### 8. Outputs and what is committed
Each run goes to `results/<what>-v<N>/` (`batch1-v1`, then `full-v1`). Committed (allow list in `results/.gitignore`): the sha256 manifest of every file of the run, `render/{run_config.json, episode_records.jsonl, timing.json, preflight.json, summary.json, episode_tallies.csv}`, `export/{export_meta.json, alignment.json}` and `env/*.txt`. Local only: poses, videos, per-step records, impulse responses, clips, pictures, logs (they hold scene coordinates or InteriorGS-derived content). Every committed file is scanned for tokens, keys, emails and home paths first.

## Predictions / success criteria (before running)
- Expected: the preflight passes (route A measured the KEMAR sign in free field); most counted steps have the correct sign, because with line of sight the direct sound is the earliest and usually the strongest arrival. Possible failure modes: strong early reflections inside the 2 ms window near walls; furniture boxes that the mesh has but the picture does not (or the reverse); the source inside another box (then no step is in line of sight).
- PASS: at least 90 percent correct over at least 200 counted steps. FAIL: below 90 percent; the per-step records and the pictures are then read before anything else is changed. TOO_FEW: not enough counted steps to say.
- A PASS says the rendered sound carries a correct left/right timing cue in the video's frame for these episodes. It says nothing about whether a model can use it, nothing about front/back, and nothing about the one-channel audio beyond its having no interaural difference.
